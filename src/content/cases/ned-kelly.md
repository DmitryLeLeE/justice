## The case

Ned Kelly led a gang of outlaws in north-eastern Victoria. After a shoot-out with police at Glenrowan in June 1880 he was captured, wounded, wearing home-made armour.

He was tried in Melbourne in October 1880 for the murder of Constable Thomas Lonigan. The Illustrated Australian News printed a wood engraving of Kelly in the dock, reproduced here. The jury found him guilty, and Judge Redmond Barry passed the death sentence. Kelly was hanged within weeks, despite a large public petition for mercy.

## In the courtroom

By the time Kelly came to trial he had been shot in the legs and captured at Glenrowan, after the gang had derailed a train and killed several people. He was tried only for the killing of Constable Lonigan. The case turned on the testimony of the surviving policeman, Constable McIntyre.

Kelly was a skilled and confident speaker, and he had written an enormous letter of grievance in 1879, but his lawyer was inexperienced. The jury took half an hour. Judge Barry's sentence of death ended with "May the Lord have mercy on your soul", and Kelly is said to have answered that he would see Barry in the next world.

## Aftermath

More than 30,000 people signed a petition for a reprieve. Kelly was hanged, and Barry died twelve days later. The Illustrated Australian News scene reproduced here appeared in the weeks after the trial.
