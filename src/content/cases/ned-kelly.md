Ned Kelly led a gang of outlaws in north-eastern Victoria. After a shoot-out with police at Glenrowan in June 1880 he was captured, wounded, wearing home-made armour.

He was tried in Melbourne in October 1880 for the murder of Constable Thomas Lonigan. The Illustrated Australian News printed a wood engraving of Kelly in the dock, reproduced here. The jury found him guilty, and Judge Redmond Barry passed the death sentence. Kelly was hanged within weeks, despite a large public petition for mercy.
