President Lincoln was shot at Ford's Theatre on 14 April 1865 and died the next morning. In the weeks that followed, authorities arrested a group of people accused of helping John Wilkes Booth plan the attack. They were tried not in a civilian court but by a military commission, which sat in the Old Arsenal Penitentiary in Washington in May and June 1865.

The courtroom was drawn by artists for Harper's Weekly and other illustrated papers, and their engravings gave the public its view of the proceedings. Four of the defendants were hanged at the penitentiary on 7 July 1865.

Whether civilians could be tried by a military commission was disputed at the time and has been argued about since.
