## The case

The Khmer Rouge governed Cambodia from 1975 to 1979 and are held responsible for the deaths of about 1.7 million people, a fifth of the population, from execution, forced labour and starvation. Kaing Guek Eav, a former maths teacher known as Duch, ran the secret prison S-21 in a former school in Phnom Penh, now the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum. At least 12,000 prisoners were brought there, tortured until they confessed to imaginary plots, and taken to the killing fields at Choeung Ek, outside the capital. Very few survived.

## In the courtroom

Duch was the first of the Khmer Rouge leaders to be tried by the tribunal that Cambodia and the United Nations set up. The trial began in February 2009 and he took responsibility for what happened at S-21 and apologised to the families; later in the proceedings he asked to be acquitted, claiming that he was not a senior leader. In July 2010 the Trial Chamber sentenced him to 35 years, cut to 19 for time already served and for unlawful detention, which angered many survivors.

## Aftermath

Both sides appealed. On 3 February 2012 the Supreme Court Chamber found that the sentence was too light and imposed life. Duch died in a Phnom Penh hospital in 2020, aged 77.

## 18+ · S-21

Prisoners at S-21 were photographed on arrival, shackled in cells, beaten and subjected to electric shocks, waterboarding and the removal of fingernails. The guards recorded their confessions and then, in most cases, took them to be killed. The prison staff photographed thousands of prisoners, men, women and children, and their archive is one of the central pieces of evidence in the case.
