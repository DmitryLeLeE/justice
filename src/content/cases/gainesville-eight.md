## The case

Eight members of Vietnam Veterans Against the War were tried in Florida on charges of conspiring to disrupt the 1972 Republican National Convention.

The jury acquitted all eight defendants in August 1973.

## In the courtroom

The government's case came from informants who had been planted among the veterans. The accused were said to have discussed using slingshots, crossbows and automatic weapons against police at the convention. The defence argued that the informants had been paid and that their accounts of the supposed plan did not hold together.

Aggie Kenny, drawing for the networks, shows the defendants and their lawyers at the table.

## Aftermath

The jury, after a short deliberation, acquitted all eight. The case followed the Chicago trial and the Camden 28 prosecution, and is usually read as a sign that jurors were by 1973 distrustful of cases built on informants and conspiracy theories. It also became part of the record of government infiltration of antiwar groups.
