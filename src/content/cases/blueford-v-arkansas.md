## The case

Alex Blueford was charged with capital murder in the death of his girlfriend's twenty-month-old son. The jury was instructed to consider capital murder first and then lesser charges in order. The foreperson told the judge the jurors were unanimous against capital murder and first-degree murder, but deadlocked on manslaughter, and the judge declared a mistrial. Blueford argued that the Double Jeopardy Clause barred the state from trying him again on the two charges on which the jury had said it agreed.

## At the Supreme Court

The Court held that the report was not a final verdict. The jurors had not been asked to vote formally on the two charges, and they could still have reconsidered them in deliberations on the lesser offence. Three justices dissented.

The drawing of the argument is credited to Dustin McDaniel on Wikimedia Commons.

## Why it matters

The case is a standard reference on what counts as an acquittal for double jeopardy purposes when a jury is split.
