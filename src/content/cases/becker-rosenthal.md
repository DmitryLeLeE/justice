## The case

Herman Rosenthal ran gambling rooms in Manhattan and complained to a newspaper that Lieutenant Charles Becker, who led an anti-vice squad, took a share of the proceeds and then had the places raided. Around 2 a.m. in July 1912 Rosenthal was shot dead outside the Hotel Metropole near Times Square. Four gangsters, Lefty Louie, Whitey Lewis, Dago Frank and Gyp the Blood, were convicted of the killing and executed. The district attorney, Charles Whitman, built his case that Becker had ordered it.

## In the courtroom

The star witness was Jack Rose, a gambler who had arranged the killing and who testified in return for immunity that Becker was behind it. Becker pleaded not guilty. He was convicted of first-degree murder in the autumn of 1912 by a jury before Judge John Goff, whose conduct of the trial was later held to be biased. The conviction was overturned on appeal because of this and because there was no independent corroboration of the accomplices' testimony.

A second jury convicted him again in 1914 and he was sentenced to death.

## Aftermath

Becker was electrocuted at Sing Sing on 30 July 1915, maintaining his innocence. Some writers have argued that he was framed by an ambitious prosecutor; others, among them the historian Thomas Reppetto, think that he was guilty. Whitman went on to become governor of New York.
