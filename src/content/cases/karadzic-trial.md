## The case

Karadžić was president of the self-declared Republika Srpska during the Bosnian war of 1992 to 1995. The Tribunal at The Hague indicted him in 1995 for his role in the siege of Sarajevo, the expulsion of non-Serbs from large parts of Bosnia and the killings at Srebrenica. He disappeared for thirteen years. In July 2008 Serbian security forces found him in Belgrade, bearded, under the name Dragan Dabić and working as a practitioner of alternative medicine.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened in October 2010 and Karadžić defended himself, with a team of legal advisers. He argued that he had never ordered the killings and that Bosnian Serbs had acted in defence. His case, with the prosecution evidence, ran for around five years. In March 2016 the judges found him guilty of genocide for Srebrenica, and of persecution, extermination, murder, deportation and hostage-taking, but not of genocide in the municipalities of Bosnia. He was sentenced to 40 years.

## Aftermath

Both sides appealed. On 20 March 2019 the Appeals Chamber of the Mechanism for International Criminal Tribunals found that the sentence did not reflect the gravity of the crimes and increased it to life imprisonment. He was later transferred to serve his sentence in the United Kingdom.

## 18+ · Sarajevo and Srebrenica

Judges found that Karadžić took part in a joint plan to remove Bosnian Muslims and Croats from Serb-claimed territory by force. In Sarajevo the siege, which lasted almost four years, brought daily shelling and sniping of civilians. After the fall of Srebrenica, around 8,000 Muslim men and boys were killed in a few days in July 1995.
