## The case

Jack Ruby shot Lee Harvey Oswald on live television two days after the assassination of President Kennedy. His trial in Dallas was the only one to come out of that weekend.

On 14 March 1964 Ruby was found guilty of murder and sentenced to death. The conviction was overturned on appeal in 1966 and he died in January 1967 before a retrial.

## In the courtroom

Ruby had killed Oswald in front of the cameras, so guilt was not in doubt. The defence of Melvin Belli, the San Francisco lawyer, argued that Ruby was mentally ill and had acted in a fit of epilepsy, and sought a change of venue because of the publicity in Dallas. The judge refused. The jury convicted him of murder with malice.

Howard Brodie drew the verdict.

## Aftermath

The state's highest criminal court overturned the conviction because the judge should have moved the trial, and a new trial was ordered for Wichita Falls. Ruby fell ill with cancer and died before it began. The Warren Commission, which interviewed him in his cell in June 1964, found no link to a conspiracy.
