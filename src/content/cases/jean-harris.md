## The case

Jean Harris, headmistress of an exclusive girls' school, was tried in White Plains for the shooting of her lover, the cardiologist Herman Tarnower.

Harris was convicted of second-degree murder on 24 February 1981. Her sentence was commuted in 1992.

## In the courtroom

Harris said she had gone to Tarnower's house to kill herself in front of him, and that the pistol went off in a struggle. The prosecution said she shot him in a jealous rage after he had left her for a younger woman, his assistant Lynne Tryforos. Harris testified at length, for days, and a letter she had written to him and posted before the shooting, long and furious, was read to the jury.

Papin's drawing is from 6 February 1981, near the end of the trial.

## Aftermath

She was convicted of second-degree murder and sentenced to 15 years to life. Her time in prison, where she taught and wrote, helped win public sympathy and she was granted clemency at the end of 1992. The trial was widely read as a story of class and of a woman who would not play the part of a victim.
