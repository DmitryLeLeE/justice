## The case

In 1929 Düsseldorf was gripped by fear. Women, men and children were attacked with knives, hammers and scissors, and some were killed. The press gave the unknown killer the name "Vampire of Düsseldorf". Anonymous letters and a flood of false leads hampered the police. The break came in May 1930, when a woman whom Kürten had taken to his flat and then let go wrote a letter that reached the police, and led them to the house. He was arrested on 24 May and confessed to the police.

## In the courtroom

The trial began on 13 April 1931. Kürten told the court what he had done, in a matter-of-fact way, and the court heard psychiatric experts. The expert Karl Berg gave evidence that he was sane. On 22 April 1931 he was found guilty of nine murders and seven attempts and sentenced to death.

## Aftermath

Kürten was guillotined at Klingelpütz prison in Cologne on 2 July 1931. His case was a landmark in forensic psychiatry in Germany and a subject for later researchers.

## 18+ · What Kürten told the court

Kürten said that he took a sexual satisfaction from violence and from the sight of blood. He admitted to killing a nine-year-old girl in 1913, to attacks on animals, and to having attacked people with a hammer in 1929 and 1930. He also said that he had killed children, and that he had once sent a newspaper a map showing where a child's body had been left.
