## The case

Roger Tichborne, heir to a baronetcy, was lost at sea in 1854. His mother, who refused to believe he was dead, advertised for him, and years later a man in Wagga Wagga, Australia, answered. The Claimant, as he is known, was heavy and uneducated and bore little resemblance to the slim, French-speaking young man who had left, but Lady Tichborne recognised him. After her death the family contested his claim.

## In the courtroom

The civil case, heard in 1871 and 1872, lasted more than a hundred days and ended when the jury said that it had heard enough. The Claimant was arrested for perjury. In the criminal trial before three judges, his counsel Edward Kenealy attacked the court and the witnesses so wildly that he was later disbarred. The jury found that the Claimant was Arthur Orton, a butcher from Wapping.

## Aftermath

He served ten years of his sentence. The case split Victorian opinion, and a radical movement grew up behind the idea that the Claimant had been wronged. In 1895 he confessed for a fee and then took it back. He died in poverty in 1898.
