## The case

On 23 August 1989 Yusuf Hawkins, a sixteen-year-old from East New York, went with friends to Bensonhurst, a mostly Italian-American neighbourhood in Brooklyn, to look at a used car. A group of white youths, who had been told a black boy was coming to see a girl in the area, surrounded them with bats, and one of them shot Hawkins dead.

## In the courtroom

Six young men were tried before Justice Thaddeus Owens in the State Supreme Court in Brooklyn in 1990. Marilyn Church drew the defendants, lined up with their lawyers before the bench and watched by a line of court officers. Joseph Fama was convicted of the shooting. Keith Mondello, said to have led the mob, was acquitted of murder and manslaughter but convicted of lesser charges, and he spent eight years in prison. Most of the other defendants were acquitted or received community service. The Library notes that this outcome divided Brooklyn.

## Aftermath

After the verdicts, Hawkins's mother Diane and the Rev. Al Sharpton reacted in court, and Sharpton led marches into Bensonhurst. Fama is still in a maximum-security prison. Church's second drawing, of the family reacting to the Mondello verdict, is held by the Library of Congress.
