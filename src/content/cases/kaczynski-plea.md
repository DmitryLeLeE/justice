## The case

Between 1978 and 1995 the FBI's "UNABOM" investigation followed a bomber who killed three people and injured 23 with devices sent by mail or left in person, many of them aimed at universities and airlines. In 1995 the bomber promised to stop if a long essay, "Industrial Society and Its Future", was printed in a major newspaper. The Washington Post published it on 19 September. David Kaczynski recognised the writing as his brother's and told the FBI, and agents arrested Theodore Kaczynski at his remote cabin near Lincoln, Montana, on 3 April 1996.

## In the courtroom

The fight in Sacramento was not over the bombings but over his mind. His lawyers, Judy Clarke and Michael Donahoe, wanted to argue that he was mentally ill, which was the best chance of avoiding the death penalty. Kaczynski, who insisted that he was sane and that his views were political, tried to dismiss them and then to represent himself. After the judge, Garland Burrell, would not allow that, he agreed to a deal: on 22 January 1998 he pleaded guilty to all charges and received life sentences without parole.

## Aftermath

He served his sentence at the supermax prison in Florence, Colorado, and later in a medical facility in North Carolina, where he died on 10 June 2023 at 81.
