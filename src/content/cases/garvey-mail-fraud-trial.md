## The case

Marcus Garvey, a Jamaican-born activist, founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association and built it into a mass movement in the United States with a message of Black pride and economic independence. In 1919 he started the Black Star Line, a shipping company financed by selling shares to Black supporters, which bought several old ships; they proved unseaworthy and the company lost money. In January 1922, the Justice Department, whose Bureau of Investigation under J. Edgar Hoover had been watching Garvey, charged him with using the mails to defraud.

## In the courtroom

The trial in the federal court in Manhattan, before Judge Julian Mack, opened in May 1923. Garvey dismissed his lawyer and spoke for himself for much of the proceedings. The three co-defendants were acquitted, but Garvey was found guilty on 23 June of one count of mail fraud and sentenced to five years and a fine.

## Aftermath

After his appeals failed, Garvey served about two years of his sentence in Atlanta, and in 1927 President Coolidge commuted it and ordered him deported. He died in London in 1940, and Jamaica has made him a national hero.
