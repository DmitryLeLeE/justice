## The case

North Korea captured the intelligence ship USS Pueblo in January 1968. The crew was released in December 1968 after long captivity.

In early 1969 a Navy court of inquiry met at Coronado, California, to establish what had happened and whether anyone should face discipline. The court recommended courts-martial for the ship's commander, Lloyd Bucher, and another officer, but the Navy Secretary dropped the matter and no one was tried. The painter Arnold Mesches made courtroom drawings of the hearing.

## In the courtroom

The court sat for about two months. Bucher, who had surrendered the ship without a fight and, under torture, signed a confession, said that resistance would have meant the loss of the whole crew. Many of the crew spoke well of him. The board nonetheless recommended courts-martial for Bucher and Harris.

Arnold Mesches, a painter who worked for the press, made the courtroom drawing shown here.

## Aftermath

Secretary Chafee said the crew had suffered enough, and the matter ended. Bucher stayed in the Navy until 1973. The Pueblo has never been returned and is moored in Pyongyang as a museum ship.
