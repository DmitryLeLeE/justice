## The case

The Bushveldt Carbineers were an irregular mounted unit raised by the British in 1901 in the Zoutpansberg district of the Transvaal to fight Boer guerrillas. Harry Morant, an Englishman who had lived in Australia as a horse-breaker and poet and who was known as "Breaker", received a lieutenant's commission in April 1901. In early August 1901 their captain, Percy Hunt, was killed in a fight at a farmhouse and his body was later found mutilated. After that the patrols were told to take no prisoners. In the weeks that followed Boer prisoners were shot, among them Floris Visser, a wounded man, and eight men near the Elim hospital, and a missionary, Carl Heese, who was travelling to report the unit's conduct, was killed.

## In the courtroom

Morant, Peter Handcock, George Witton and others were tried by court-martial at Pietersburg in early 1902. The defence was led by Major James Thomas, a solicitor from New South Wales, who argued that the men had acted on orders not to take prisoners. He could not prove that such an order had been given. The court convicted Morant of murder in several killings and convicted Handcock, and acquitted both of killing Heese. Witton was also sentenced to death.

## Aftermath

Lord Kitchener confirmed the death sentences for Morant and Handcock, and both were shot by a firing squad of Cameron Highlanders at Pretoria on 27 February 1902. Witton's sentence was commuted to life, and he was released in 1904. In Australia the two men are widely seen as scapegoats of a British effort to appease the Boers, and campaigners continue to ask for a pardon or a new trial.
