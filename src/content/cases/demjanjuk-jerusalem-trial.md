## The case

John Demjanjuk, born in Ukraine, was a retired autoworker in Cleveland, Ohio. Survivors of the Treblinka camp had named a guard who operated the gas chambers as "Ivan the Terrible", and identified Demjanjuk from photographs as that man. He was deported to Israel on 28 February 1986 to stand trial under the law used against Adolf Eichmann.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened in Jerusalem in February 1987 before three judges, Dov Levin, Zvi Tal and Dalia Dorner, and was held in a theatre hall so that the public could attend. Survivors identified Demjanjuk in the courtroom, and the prosecution relied on an identity card from the Trawniki training camp bearing his name. The defence said that the card was a forgery and that the survivors had been wrong after four decades. On 18 April 1988 the court found him guilty, and sentenced him to death a week later.

## Aftermath

Demjanjuk appealed. After the fall of the Soviet Union, documents from its archives suggested that Ivan the Terrible had been another man, and on 29 July 1993 the Supreme Court of Israel set the verdict aside for reasonable doubt, while noting that he had served at other camps. He returned to the United States. In Germany he was tried in Munich from November 2009 as a guard at the Sobibor camp, and on 12 May 2011 he was convicted as an accessory to the murder of 28,060 people and sentenced to five years. He died on 17 March 2012 at a nursing home in Bavaria, before his appeal was decided.
