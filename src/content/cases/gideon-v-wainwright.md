## The case

In June 1961 a poolroom in Panama City, Florida, was broken into and money was taken from a vending machine. Clarence Earl Gideon, a 51-year-old with a record of small crimes, was arrested. At his trial in August he asked the judge to appoint a lawyer. The judge refused, because under Florida law that was permitted only in capital cases. Gideon defended himself poorly, was convicted and was sentenced to five years.

## In the courtroom

From the Florida State Prison he wrote out a petition by hand on prison stationery, arguing that the Constitution gave him a right to a lawyer. The Supreme Court took the case, which was unusual, and appointed a prominent Washington lawyer, Abe Fortas, to argue it. On 18 March 1963 the Court decided unanimously, in an opinion by Justice Hugo Black, that the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies in state courts to all those charged with serious crimes who cannot afford a lawyer.

## Aftermath

Gideon was retried in August 1963 with a lawyer, who exposed weaknesses in the main prosecution witness, and the jury acquitted him. The decision forced the states to build public defender systems, and the law of the right to counsel is still being tested in those courts.
