## The case

Warren Hastings had governed Bengal for the East India Company from 1773, first as Governor and then as the first Governor-General, and had expanded the Company's power through wars and the exaction of money from Indian rulers. Edmund Burke had been attacking his conduct for years. After Hastings returned to England in 1785, the House of Commons voted articles of impeachment, accusing him among other things of taking bribes and of extorting money from the Raja of Benares and from the Begums of Awadh.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened in Westminster Hall on 13 February 1788 before the House of Lords with Burke opening for the Commons in a speech that lasted several days. The managers included Charles James Fox and the playwright Richard Brinsley Sheridan, whose speech on the Begums of Awadh was so admired that the Commons adjourned to let members recover. The proceedings dragged on, with long gaps, for seven years, and by the end many of the peers who had heard the opening had died or lost interest.

## Aftermath

The Lords voted on 23 April 1795, and acquitted Hastings of all charges by large majorities. His legal costs had been enormous and the East India Company later awarded him a pension. The trial helped to establish the idea that British rule in India carried a duty to the governed, and it remains the longest impeachment in British history.
