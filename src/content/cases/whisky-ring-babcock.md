## The case

Between 1871 and 1875 distillers in St. Louis and other cities paid Treasury officials to let them avoid the tax on whisky, and some of the money went to the Republican Party. When Treasury Secretary Benjamin Bristow raided the distilleries in 1875, the evidence reached the White House, where Babcock was accused of warning the ring. Of 238 people indicted, 110 were convicted.

## In the courtroom

Babcock's trial lasted eighteen days. The special prosecutor, John B. Henderson, had been removed from the case after he criticised Grant's interference, and a new prosecutor took over. Grant, who believed in his secretary, gave a deposition that was read to the jury and vouched for his integrity.

## Aftermath

The jury acquitted Babcock. The affair damaged the standing of Grant's administration. McDonald was convicted and sentenced to eighteen months, and Grant pardoned him in March 1877.
