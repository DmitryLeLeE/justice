## The case

David Nicoll edited Commonweal, the paper of the Socialist League, and Charles Mowbray published it. In April 1892 Nicoll printed an article that asked whether the judge Henry Hawkins, the Home Secretary Henry Matthews and the Special Branch inspector William Melville were fit to live. The authorities took it as an incitement to murder.

## In the courtroom

The Attorney General himself led the prosecution. Nicoll chose to defend himself. Mowbray was acquitted because he had not been involved in producing that issue. Nicoll was sentenced to eighteen months with hard labour.

The sketch was made in court during the proceedings.

## Aftermath

He served his term at Chelmsford, partly under medical supervision, and when he came out a large crowd met him in London and there were clashes with the police. The Commonweal group never gave him back his editorship, and the paper declined. His health failed, and he died in poverty in a London hospital in 1919.
