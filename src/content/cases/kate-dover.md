## The case

Dover had kept house for Thomas Skinner, a Sheffield etcher and inventor, since 1880. On 6 December 1881 she served him a roast dinner with arsenic in the stuffing, and he died. She was arrested twelve days later.

## In the courtroom

The trial was held in Leeds Town Hall before Mr Justice Cave. Frank Lockwood, her counsel, argued that she had meant to make Skinner ill so that his former housekeeper would be blamed, and had not meant to kill him. The jury convicted her of manslaughter, not murder. Cave sentenced her to penal servitude for life and said that her offence was separated from murder only by a very thin line.

## Aftermath

She served her sentence at Woking Female Prison and was freed some time after 1895. She lived with her sister in Rotherham and died unmarried in 1925.
