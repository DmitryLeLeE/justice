## The case

Bernard Madoff pleaded guilty in March 2009 to running a Ponzi scheme that defrauded investors of billions of dollars. The drawing shows his sentencing in June 2009.

Madoff was sentenced to 150 years in prison on 29 June 2009.

## In the courtroom

There was no trial, since Madoff had already pleaded guilty. The sentencing hearing was where his victims could speak. The Library holds a drawing of the day by Kenny. Several people spoke of ruined retirements and of the shame of having trusted him. Madoff apologised, saying "I live in a tormented state", and that he left "a legacy of shame". Judge Chin said the crime was "extraordinarily evil" and gave him the maximum.

## Aftermath

The scheme had been running for decades, with promised returns that were never real. It was valued at about $65 billion on the paper statements, with real losses of around $17 billion in invested money. A trustee has since recovered a large part. Madoff died in prison in 2021, and his long-time associates were convicted in a later trial.
