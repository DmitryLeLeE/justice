## The case

Aldrich Ames joined the CIA in 1962 and rose to lead a counterintelligence unit working against the Soviet Union. In April 1985, short of money, he walked into the Soviet embassy in Washington and offered to sell the names of CIA sources. Over the next nine years he received about $4.6 million. At least ten sources that he betrayed were executed, and more than a hundred US intelligence operations were compromised.

The CIA took years to see a pattern, even though Ames bought a house and a Jaguar on a salary that did not support them. After a long investigation he and his wife Rosario were arrested on 21 February 1994.

## In the courtroom

There was no trial. Ames pleaded guilty to espionage and tax evasion, and in the Alexandria courthouse on 28 April 1994 Judge Claude Hilton sentenced him to life in prison without parole. Rosario, who had known about the payments and helped to conceal them, was sentenced to five years.

## Aftermath

The affair led to a bruising row over the CIA's management when Director James Woolsey decided that no one would lose their job, and Woolsey later resigned. Congress gave the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court the power to authorise physical searches for intelligence purposes. Ames died in a Maryland federal prison in January 2026, aged 84.
