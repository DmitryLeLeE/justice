## The case

Whitey Bulger led the Winter Hill Gang in Boston from the 1970s and, for years, served as an FBI informant, which gave him inside information on investigations. His FBI handler, John Connolly, was later convicted of racketeering and murder for helping the gang. Bulger fled in January 1995 when he was tipped off about an indictment, spent 16 years on the FBI's most-wanted list, and was found with his partner, Catherine Greig, in an apartment in Santa Monica in June 2011, with a large cache of cash and guns.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened on 4 June 2013 in Boston before Judge Denise Casper. Prosecutors presented the evidence of former associates who had made deals, among them Kevin Weeks and John Martorano. The defence said that those witnesses were lying for their own advantage and that Bulger had not killed anyone, and, in an unusual twist, that he had never been an informant. The jury found him guilty on 31 of 32 counts and decided that he took part in eleven of the nineteen killings charged.

## Aftermath

Bulger was sentenced to two life terms plus five years. On 30 October 2018, the day after he arrived at the penitentiary in West Virginia, other inmates beat him to death. The case inspired the 2015 film Black Mass.
