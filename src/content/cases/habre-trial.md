## The case

Hissène Habré ruled Chad from 1982 until he was driven out by Idriss Déby's rebels in 1990. A Chadian commission of inquiry later estimated that about 40,000 people were killed under his rule, many of them in the secret police's prisons, where torture was routine. He fled to Cameroon and then to Senegal, where he lived in Dakar for more than twenty years. A group of victims, led by Souleymane Guengueng, who had survived prison, and supported by Human Rights Watch, pursued him through the courts of Senegal, Belgium and the African Union.

## In the courtroom

In December 2012 Senegal passed a law creating a special court, the Extraordinary African Chambers, with African judges, within its own court system. Habré was arrested on 30 June 2013, and the trial opened on 20 July 2015. He refused to recognise the court, shouted at the judges, and his lawyers refused to take part, so lawyers were appointed for him. On 30 May 2016 the court found him guilty and sentenced him to life. The convictions covered rape and sexual slavery and ordering killings.

## Aftermath

In 2017 the appeal chamber upheld the life sentence and most of the convictions, setting aside the rape conviction on procedural grounds. It was the first time that the courts of one country had tried a former head of state of another for human rights abuses on the basis of universal jurisdiction. Habré died of Covid-19 in Dakar on 24 August 2021.
