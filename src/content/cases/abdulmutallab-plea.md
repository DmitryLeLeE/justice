## The case

On 25 December 2009 Umar Farouk Abdulmutallab, a 23-year-old Nigerian who had studied in London, boarded Northwest Airlines Flight 253 in Amsterdam, carrying a sewn-in package of the explosive PETN. As the plane approached Detroit he tried to set it off with a syringe of chemicals. The device caught fire instead of exploding, passengers and a crew member overpowered him, and the plane landed safely. Al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula said that it had trained him and equipped him.

## In the courtroom

Abdulmutallab at first rejected his lawyers, dismissed the court's authority and chose to represent himself, with a stand-by attorney. His trial opened in October 2011 before Judge Nancy Edmunds and, soon after the prosecution's opening statement, he said that he wished to plead guilty to all counts. He told the judge that the attempt was a holy war in retaliation for the killing of Muslims, and that he had been prepared to die.

## Aftermath

He was sentenced in February 2012 to life in prison, in effect four consecutive life terms and an additional 50 years. His treatment as a criminal defendant, not as an enemy combatant, was criticised by some politicians at the time. The case led to changes in airport screening, including the use of body scanners.
