## The case

In December 1894 Dreyfus was convicted behind closed doors and sentenced to life in exile for passing secrets to Germany. Lieutenant-Colonel Picquart later concluded that the document on which the captain was convicted had been written by Major Esterhazy. Esterhazy was acquitted, and in January 1898 Émile Zola published the open letter "J'accuse". In June 1899 the Court of Cassation ordered a new trial.

Dreyfus was brought back from Devil's Island to France. The second court martial sat in Rennes from 7 August to 9 September 1899, far from the unrest in Paris. Even contemporaries found the verdict of treason "with extenuating circumstances" strange.

Dreyfus dropped his appeal to obtain a pardon faster. The Court of Cassation closed the case in 1906, ruling that a key document in the prosecution file had been forged and that no new trial was needed. Dreyfus was then reinstated in the army.

## In the courtroom

The trial was held in the hall of a boys' school in Rennes, under heavy guard. The public saw a Dreyfus aged by years on Devil's Island, who could hardly keep upright and spoke in a broken voice. His lawyer Labori was shot in the back on his way to court; he returned a week later.

The army again produced its case against him, and General Mercier and other senior officers gave their evidence in uniform. The judges, all serving officers, seemed unable to say the army had been wrong. The finding of "extenuating circumstances", which makes no sense in a treason case, was a way of giving something to both sides.

## Aftermath

The pardon came ten days later, when Dreyfus accepted it to end the ordeal. Only in 1906 did the Court of Cassation clear him without sending the case to a new trial. He was reinstated in the army and made a knight of the Legion of Honour in a courtyard of the École militaire.
