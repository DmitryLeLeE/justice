## The case

Maximilian, a younger brother of the Austrian emperor Franz Joseph, accepted the crown of Mexico in 1864 from Mexican conservatives, with the backing of Napoleon III, whose army had occupied the country. Benito Juárez's republican government never gave up the fight, and when the French withdrew in 1866 and 1867 the empire collapsed. Maximilian made his last stand in Querétaro. The city fell on 15 May 1867, and he was taken prisoner the next day with the generals Miguel Miramón and Tomás Mejía.

## In the courtroom

The three were tried by a military court that opened on 13 June in the Teatro Iturbide in Querétaro. Maximilian, who was unwell, did not appear. His lawyers, Mariano Riva Palacio and Rafael Martínez de la Torre, defended the legitimacy of the empire. The charges included conspiring against the republic and carrying out the "Black Decree", under which captured republican fighters had been shot. The court sentenced all three to death.

## Aftermath

Appeals for mercy came from many parts of Europe, among them Victor Hugo and Giuseppe Garibaldi, but Juárez refused, in part to show that Mexico would not tolerate a new foreign intervention. The three were shot at Cerro de las Campanas on 19 June 1867. Édouard Manet made the execution the subject of several paintings, among them the one shown here.
