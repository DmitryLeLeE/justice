## The case

In the early 1980s the Sicilian Mafia was fighting a war that left hundreds dead. Judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino of the Palermo anti-Mafia pool built a case on the testimony of Tommaso Buscetta, a senior Mafioso who had fled to Brazil, was extradited and decided to talk in 1984; others followed. The indictment named hundreds of defendants, with a record of killings and drug deals that tied the "Commission" that ran Cosa Nostra to the crimes of its members.

## In the courtroom

A heavily guarded courtroom was built beside the Ucciardone prison in Palermo, with cages for the accused. The trial opened in February 1986 under Judge Alfonso Giordano and ran almost two years. Buscetta's testimony on how the Mafia was organised was the heart of the case. On 16 December 1987 the court handed down 19 life sentences and sentences totalling 2,665 years, and acquitted many of the other defendants.

## Aftermath

The sentences were largely overturned on appeal in 1990, but the Court of Cassation in Rome restored them on 30 January 1992. The Mafia responded by killing Falcone, with his wife and three bodyguards, in a bomb attack near Palermo on 23 May 1992, and Borsellino in another on 19 July. The killings shocked Italy and led to a large crackdown.
