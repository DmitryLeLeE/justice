## The case

Gilbert Hyatt, an inventor who held a patent related to the microprocessor, reported in 1991 that he had moved from California to Nevada, with the effect that he would owe no California income tax on large licensing income. California audited him and claimed he had stayed in the state. Hyatt sued the tax agency in Nevada for the way the audit had been conducted.

## At the Supreme Court

The case reached the Court three times. In the third round, argued by Seth Waxman for Hyatt in January 2019, the question was whether the Court should overrule Nevada v. Hall, which had allowed a State to be sued in another State's courts. Justice Thomas, for a five-justice majority, held that the Constitution's structure keeps the States' immunity from private suits in the courts of other States, and overruled Hall. The four dissenters, led by Justice Breyer, warned that the Court was giving up a rule of long standing.

The sketch by Arthur Lien shows Waxman at the lectern.

## Aftermath

The decision became part of a line of Supreme Court rulings on state sovereign immunity.
