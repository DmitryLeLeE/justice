## The case

Caylee Anthony, who was two, was last seen in June 2008. Her mother, Casey, did not report her missing for about a month, and in the meantime told her family and the police an invented story about a nanny. After the child's grandmother called the police in July, Casey was arrested for lying and neglect. In December a meter reader found a child's remains in woods near the family's home in Orlando.

## In the courtroom

The trial, held in May and June 2011 before Judge Belvin Perry Jr., was broadcast live and followed closely. The prosecution said that Casey had suffocated the child with duct tape so that she could have a free life, and pointed to the smell in the car and her partying in the weeks that followed. The defence, led by José Baez, said that Caylee had drowned in the family's swimming pool and that her father, George, had helped to hide the body, an accusation that he denied. The state could not show a cause of death.

After about eleven hours of deliberation the jury found her not guilty of murder and of the two other main charges, and guilty of four misdemeanours for lying to police.

## Aftermath

She was sentenced to four years in jail, with credit for time served, and was freed on 17 July 2011. The verdict set off public anger, and Florida passed "Caylee's Law" making it a crime for a parent to fail to report a missing child.
