Mary Phagan, a 13-year-old worker at an Atlanta pencil factory, was found dead in April 1913. Leo Frank, the factory's superintendent, was charged with her murder.

The trial opened at the end of July 1913 in a courtroom packed with spectators, with crowds outside. The prosecution's case rested heavily on the testimony of a factory worker, Jim Conley, and Frank was convicted.

Appeals failed, but Governor John Slaton reviewed the evidence and commuted the sentence in June 1915. In August 1915 a group of men took Frank from prison and lynched him. In 1986 the Georgia Board of Pardons and Paroles granted him a posthumous pardon, without ruling on his guilt.
