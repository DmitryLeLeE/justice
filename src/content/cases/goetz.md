## The case

Bernhard Goetz shot four young men on a New York subway train in December 1984. The case divided the city over self-defence and fear of crime.

In June 1987 a jury acquitted Goetz of attempted murder and convicted him only of illegal gun possession. He served about eight months.

## In the courtroom

Goetz had been mugged before, and on the afternoon of 22 December 1984 four youths, one of whom was carrying a sharpened screwdriver, approached him on a train and asked for five dollars. He drew a pistol and fired five shots. The defence said that he reasonably believed he was about to be robbed or hurt. The prosecution said that he was a man who had been looking for a chance and fired a second shot at one boy, Darrell Cabey, saying "You seem to be all right, here's another".

The jury, which had been shown Goetz's taped statement, decided that his fear was reasonable. They convicted him of carrying an unlicensed loaded gun.

## Aftermath

The case became a symbol of a frightened New York and of the argument over self-defence. Goetz served eight months. Cabey, who was left paralysed, won a $43 million civil judgment against him in 1996.
