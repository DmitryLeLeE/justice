## The case

Rudolf Abel was a Soviet colonel who ran an illegal spy network in the United States from a Brooklyn studio, where he lived as a painter and photographer. His assistant, Reino Häyhänen, who had grown bored and careless with his money and drink, defected to the Americans in 1957 and named him. The FBI arrested Abel on 21 June in a hotel room in Manhattan, where he was staying under another name, and he refused to speak.

## In the courtroom

James Donovan, an insurance lawyer who had served as counsel at the Nuremberg trials, was named to defend him. He challenged the way the FBI had searched the studio and argued that the case rested on the word of Häyhänen, who had admitted to lying. The jury convicted Abel on 25 October 1957. The espionage conspiracy count carried the death penalty. Donovan, thinking that a live Soviet spy might one day be exchanged for an American, urged the judge to spare him, and Judge Byers imposed 30 years on 15 November.

## Aftermath

The Supreme Court upheld the conviction by 5 to 4 in 1960, a ruling on the legality of the search. In February 1962 Donovan negotiated the exchange of Abel for Francis Gary Powers, whose U-2 spy plane had been shot down over the Soviet Union in 1960, on the Glienicke Bridge between East and West Berlin. The 2015 film Bridge of Spies tells the story.
