## The case

Andrew Borden, a wealthy banker and manufacturer, and his second wife Abby were killed with a hatchet or axe on a hot August morning in 1892. Lizzie, his daughter by his first marriage, was at home, with the maid Bridget Sullivan. The police had no other suspect and no weapon that could be tied to her, and the evidence against her was circumstantial.

## In the courtroom

The trial ran for two weeks before three judges in New Bedford, with reporters from across the country in the room. The prosecution said that Lizzie had a quarrel with her stepmother and a motive in the inheritance. Her own testimony at the inquest, which was inconsistent, was kept from the jury by the court. The defence stressed that no blood had been found on her clothes and no one had seen her do it.

## Aftermath

The jury acquitted her after deliberating for under two hours. She remained in Fall River, in a large house she called Maplecroft, where she was shunned by many neighbours, and she died in 1927. The case lives on in a children's rhyme and in countless retellings.

## 18+ · The killings

Abby Borden was struck about eighteen or nineteen times with a hatchet in an upstairs bedroom, and Andrew Borden about ten or eleven times as he lay on a sofa downstairs, an hour or so later. Photographs of the bodies and the victims' skulls were shown in court.
