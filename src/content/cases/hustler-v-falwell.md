## The case

The Supreme Court heard Larry Flynt's appeal against a verdict for the Rev. Jerry Falwell, whom Hustler magazine had mocked in a parody advertisement.

On 24 February 1988 the Court ruled 8-0 that a public figure cannot recover damages for emotional distress from a parody.

## At the Supreme Court

The parody presented Falwell as describing his first sexual experience as a drunken encounter with his mother in an outhouse. The jury found that no reasonable reader would take it as fact, so it rejected the libel claim, but it gave damages for emotional distress. Kenny's drawing shows the argument in the Court.

## The ruling

Rehnquist wrote for the Court that the First Amendment bars a public figure from recovering for emotional distress caused by a publication unless it contains a false statement of fact made with actual malice, and that the cartoon was outside that category. He noted the long tradition of political cartoons. The case is a standard citation for the protection of parody and satire, and was the subject of the film "The People vs. Larry Flynt" in 1996.
