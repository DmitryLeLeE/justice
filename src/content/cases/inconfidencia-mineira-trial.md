## The case

In the 1780s the gold of the captaincy of Minas Gerais, in the interior of Brazil, was running out, and Portugal demanded the same quota of gold in tax regardless. If the quota was not met, a levy called the derrama would be imposed to make up the difference. A group of landowners, officers, priests and poets, among them the poet Tomás Antônio Gonzaga, and the army ensign and dentist Joaquim José da Silva Xavier, called Tiradentes, "tooth-puller", planned to rise on the day that the derrama was declared and to set up a republic. One of the conspirators, Joaquim Silvério dos Reis, informed on the others, and they were arrested in 1789.

## In the courtroom

The prisoners were held in Rio de Janeiro and questioned over almost three years in an inquiry ordered by the viceroy, and tried by a special court. Most denied the charges or blamed others. Tiradentes took the whole responsibility on himself. On 18 April 1792 the court sentenced eleven men to hang, among them Tiradentes, Freire de Andrade and José Álvares Maciel; seven others were banished to Africa and the rest acquitted.

## Aftermath

Queen Maria I commuted the death sentences of all except Tiradentes, who was hanged in Rio on 21 April 1792, and his body was dismembered and displayed along the road to Vila Rica. After the Brazilian republic was proclaimed in 1889 he was made a national hero, and 21 April, the anniversary of his death, is a public holiday in Brazil.
