## The case

On 4 May 1886, during a strike for an eight-hour working day, a crowd met in Haymarket Square in Chicago to protest the killing of strikers by police the previous day. As police moved in to disperse the meeting, someone threw a dynamite bomb. It killed one officer, Mathias Degan, and a further six officers died later; police fired into the crowd and an unknown number of workers were killed or wounded. The police arrested the leading anarchists in the city.

## In the courtroom

Eight men were tried together for murder before Judge Joseph Gary, in a trial that began on 21 June 1886. The prosecution did not claim that any of them had thrown the bomb, which was never traced. It argued that their speeches and writings had incited the act, so that all were responsible. Several of the defendants, Albert Parsons among them, were not at the square when the bomb was thrown. The jury was picked from a pool that included men who said that they were prejudiced, and the verdict on 20 August found all eight guilty: seven were sentenced to death and Oscar Neebe to fifteen years.

## Aftermath

The Illinois Supreme Court upheld the convictions and the US Supreme Court refused to hear the case. The governor commuted the sentences of Fielden and Schwab. Louis Lingg killed himself in his cell the day before the executions, and Spies, Parsons, Fischer and Engel were hanged on 11 November 1887. In 1893 Governor John Altgeld pardoned the three survivors, saying that the jury had been packed and the judge biased. The case is remembered in May Day, which is observed in much of the world in memory of the Haymarket events.
