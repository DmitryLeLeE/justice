## The case

In May 1857 mutinous soldiers of the East India Company's army marched into Delhi and proclaimed the elderly Bahadur Shah Zafar their emperor. He was the last of the Mughal line, a ruler in name only, living on a pension inside the Red Fort and known as a poet. The rising failed. The British retook the city in September 1857, and Zafar surrendered to Captain William Hodson on his promise that his life would be spared.

## In the courtroom

The British decided to try him in Delhi, not Calcutta, and the hearing took place in the Red Fort itself, the first case ever tried there. It lasted 21 days, with 19 sittings and 21 witnesses. The charges were that he had helped others to wage war against the government, had claimed sovereignty over Hindustan, and had been an accessory to the murder of Christians. On the 20th day Zafar spoke in his own defence, saying that he had been helpless in the hands of the soldiers.

## Aftermath

Hodson's promise was honoured, and the king was not hanged. On 7 October 1858 he left Delhi in bullock carts under escort for Rangoon, where he died on 7 November 1862 and was buried near the Shwedagon Pagoda. The trial ended the Mughal dynasty, and the Crown took over the government of India from the Company.
