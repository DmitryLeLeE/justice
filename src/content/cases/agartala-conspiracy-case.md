## The case

In January 1968 the government of Pakistan under Field Marshal Ayub Khan announced that it had uncovered a plot in which Bengali officers and civil servants had met Indian officials at Agartala, in India, to plan the secession of East Pakistan. Thirty-five people were named. Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, leader of the Awami League and author of a six-point plan for greater autonomy for East Pakistan, was added to the list as the chief accused while already in prison.

## In the courtroom

A special tribunal of three judges, led by Justice S. A. Rahman, began hearing the case in June 1968 inside the Dhaka Cantonment, under army guard. The prosecution was led by the Attorney General and the former foreign minister Manzur Quader. The defence engaged a British lawyer, Thomas Williams, together with Bengali counsel. Bengalis took the case as an attempt to silence their demands for autonomy. On 15 February 1969 one of the accused, Sergeant Zahurul Haq, was shot dead in custody, and the protests grew into a general uprising.

## Aftermath

On 22 February 1969 the government withdrew the case, and the accused were freed the following day. At a huge reception at the Race Course Maidan in Dhaka, Mujib was given the title Bangabandhu. He won the election of 1970, and after the war of 1971 East Pakistan became the independent state of Bangladesh, with Mujib as its founding leader.
