## The case

In June 1935 Governor Herbert Lehman appointed Thomas Dewey, a young federal prosecutor, as special prosecutor to go after organised crime in New York. Dewey's staff raided brothels around the city and persuaded some of the women to testify against the men who ran the business. In March 1936 Luciano fled to Hot Springs, Arkansas, where he was arrested in April on 90 counts of compulsory prostitution, and then returned to New York.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened on 11 May 1936, with Dewey acting as prosecutor himself against Luciano and twelve co-defendants. Four women testified that Luciano controlled the business, among them Nancy Presser and "Cokey Flo" Brown; they said that they had been sold into or forced to remain in it. On 7 June Luciano and eight of the others were convicted on 62 counts each, and on 18 June Luciano was sentenced to 30 to 50 years. All four of the women later recanted.

## Aftermath

He served his sentence at Sing Sing and then in the Clinton prison in Dannemora. During the Second World War, the Navy asked for Mafia help guarding the New York waterfront; Luciano's later claim to have supplied useful assistance is disputed. Dewey, by then governor, commuted the sentence on 3 January 1946 on condition that Luciano did not resist deportation. He arrived in Naples on 28 February and died of a heart attack at Naples airport on 26 January 1962.
