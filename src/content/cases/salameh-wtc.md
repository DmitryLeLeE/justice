## The case

Mohammed Salameh and other defendants were tried in New York for the February 1993 truck bombing of the World Trade Center.

Salameh and three others were convicted in March 1994. Each was sentenced to 240 years in prison.

## In the courtroom

The case began with a vehicle identification number. A fragment of the Ryder van's chassis, found in the rubble, led investigators to the rental office, and then to Salameh, who went back to ask for his deposit. The trial lasted about six months in a courtroom guarded with unusual security.

The prosecution tied the four men to the purchase of the chemicals and the rented van, and to an apartment in New Jersey where the bomb was assembled. The jury deliberated for several days.

## Aftermath

The bomb killed six people and injured more than a thousand. Ramzi Yousef, the planner, had left the country and was caught in Pakistan in 1995; he was tried separately in 1996–97. The case was an early warning of what later happened at the same site on 11 September 2001.
