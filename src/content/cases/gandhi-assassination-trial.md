## The case

On 30 January 1948, as Mahatma Gandhi walked to his evening prayer meeting at Birla House in New Delhi, Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist from Pune, stepped forward, bowed, and shot him three times at close range. Godse did not run, and he was seized by the crowd. A bomb had been thrown at a prayer meeting ten days before by one of his co-conspirators, Madan Lal Pahwa, a failure which should have alerted the police. The conspirators held Gandhi responsible for the partition of India and for his insistence that Pakistan be paid its share of the treasury.

## In the courtroom

A special court sat in the Red Fort, with Judge Atma Charan presiding, from May 1948. Nine men were charged; Digambar Badge, who had supplied weapons, turned approver and gave the main evidence, and the court acquitted Vinayak Damodar Savarkar, the Hindu nationalist leader whom Badge named as the inspiration, since his word was not corroborated. Godse did not deny the shooting and spoke for several hours in court to justify it. The court sentenced Godse and Narayan Apte to death and the rest to life.

## Aftermath

The High Court at Shimla confirmed the death sentences, and Godse and Apte were hanged on 15 November 1949 in Ambala jail; two of the life sentences were set aside on appeal. Gandhi's sons had asked for clemency for Godse. The Kapur Commission, set up in 1966, reviewed the failures of security and concluded that the facts pointed to a conspiracy by Savarkar and his group, a conclusion that his supporters dispute.
