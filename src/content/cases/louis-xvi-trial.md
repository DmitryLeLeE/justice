## The case

The monarchy fell on 10 August 1792, and in September the Convention proclaimed a republic. In November a cache of papers found in an iron chest in the Tuileries showed that Louis had been in secret contact with foreign courts and with émigrés. Radical deputies, among them Saint-Just and Robespierre, argued that the king could not be tried at all, because he was the enemy of the nation, and should simply be punished. The Convention decided instead to judge him itself.

## In the courtroom

The king was brought before the Convention on 11 December 1792 and questioned about his actions. His defence was entrusted to three men, Tronchet, Malesherbes and the young Raymond de Sèze, who read the main speech on 26 December and argued that the constitution of 1791 made the king inviolable. The deputies voted on 15 January, and a very large majority found him guilty; none voted that he was innocent. The vote on the sentence on 16 and 17 January lasted around 24 hours, with each deputy speaking from the tribune; a narrow majority chose death, and a proposal for a reprieve was defeated.

## Aftermath

Louis was guillotined on 21 January 1793. His death helped to bring the monarchies of Europe into the war against France, and the Terror followed within months.
