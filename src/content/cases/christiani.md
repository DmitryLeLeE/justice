## The case

On 4 June 1899 Baron Christiani hit President Émile Loubet with a cane at the Auteuil races, in protest at the government's move to reopen the Dreyfus case. The incident became a symbol of how far the affair had polarised France.

Le Monde illustré published a sketch of him by Louis Malteste on 17 June 1899. Malteste also drew other hearings of the Dreyfus era.

## The incident

On 4 June 1899, at the Grand Steeple-Chase at Auteuil, a nationalist aristocrat in the stands hit President Émile Loubet on the head with his cane. The blow knocked off the president's hat and caused little harm. The context mattered more than the injury: the retrial of Alfred Dreyfus was coming, and Loubet was seen by the anti-Dreyfusard right as the man who had let it happen.

## In court

The case was heard quickly. The courtroom drawings, among them Malteste's in the Monde illustré, show the baron in the dock, a small and angry figure. The court treated the act as a political stunt that had gone too far and convicted him, and the Republic, which had just seen an attempted coup in February, did not intend to be lenient.

## Why it matters

The attack was one of a run of nationalist provocations in 1899 that ended in the High Court trial of the leaders of the leagues. It also shows how a case with no victim to speak of could still carry weight, because the real question was who ran France.
