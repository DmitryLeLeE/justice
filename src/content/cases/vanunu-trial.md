## The case

Mordechai Vanunu, who was born in Morocco and grew up in Israel, worked as a technician at the Negev Nuclear Research Center near Dimona from 1977 until he left in October 1985. He took photographs inside the plant and gave them, with an account of what he had seen, to the London Sunday Times, which published them on 5 October 1986 and concluded that Israel had a large nuclear arsenal. Israel has never confirmed or denied having nuclear weapons.

## In the courtroom

Before publication a Mossad agent posing as an American tourist, "Cindy", lured Vanunu from London to Rome on 30 September 1986. He was drugged and taken to Israel by ship. Israel announced on 9 November that it was holding him. He was tried in secret at the Jerusalem District Court, and the proceedings were closed to the public and the press. In March 1988 he was convicted of treason and aggravated espionage and sentenced to 18 years.

## Aftermath

Vanunu served the whole sentence, over eleven years of it in solitary confinement, and was released on 21 April 2004. He was barred from leaving Israel and from speaking to foreigners, and was arrested and prosecuted several times for breaking these conditions. To many abroad he is a whistleblower, and in Israel he is widely seen as a traitor.
