## The case

Johnson, who had succeeded Lincoln, was in open war with the Radical Republicans who controlled Congress over the terms of Reconstruction. The Tenure of Office Act of 1867 forbade him to remove cabinet members without the Senate's consent. When he dismissed Stanton, who was allied with Congress, the House voted to impeach him almost at once.

## In the courtroom

Chase presided over a Senate chamber packed with spectators who needed tickets. The managers argued that Johnson had broken the law; the defence said that the Act did not cover Stanton, who had been appointed by Lincoln, and that Johnson was testing its constitutionality. Johnson did not appear himself.

Theodore R. Davis, a special artist for Harper's Weekly, drew the chamber.

## Aftermath

Seven Republicans voted with the Democrats to acquit, and the vote was one short of conviction on the three articles the Senate took up. The acquittal set a precedent for the independence of the executive from congressional removal threats, and the Tenure of Office Act was largely repealed in 1887.
