After the war Eichmann hid in Argentina. In May 1960 Israeli intelligence agents seized him and took him to Israel. All counts rested on the 1950 law on the punishment of Nazis and their collaborators.

The hearings opened on 11 April 1961 in Beit Ha'am, a new community centre in central Jerusalem converted into a courtroom for several hundred spectators. The defendant sat in a glass booth. Eichmann said he had only followed orders.

Witness testimony ended in mid-August and the verdict was read in December. The philosopher Hannah Arendt wrote a book on the trial, "Eichmann in Jerusalem", and its conclusions are still debated.
