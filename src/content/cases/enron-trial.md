## The case

Kenneth Lay and Jeffrey Skilling were tried in Houston over the collapse of Enron.

Both men were convicted in May 2006.

## In the courtroom

Prosecutors said the two men had hidden Enron's losses behind partnerships and lied to investors about the health of the company. The defence said Enron was sound and that its collapse came from a loss of confidence, driven by the press and short-sellers. Both men testified in their own defence.

The jury, in Houston, found Skilling guilty on 19 of 28 counts and Lay guilty on all six. He was acquitted on nine counts, all of them insider-trading charges.

## Aftermath

Lay died six weeks later, before sentencing, and under the rule then used in the Fifth Circuit his conviction was erased. Skilling was sentenced to 24 years and four months, later cut to 14 years on appeal, and was released in 2019. Enron's auditor, Arthur Andersen, did not survive its own conviction. The scandal led to the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002.
