## The case

Michael Jackson was tried in Santa Maria, California, on child molestation charges.

The jury acquitted Jackson on all counts in June 2005.

## In the courtroom

The trial lasted nearly five months. The accuser, a boy who had been treated for cancer and had stayed at Neverland Ranch, testified, as did his family. The defence said the family had a history of seeking money from celebrities and that the account was inconsistent. Jackson did not testify, and his attorney Thomas Mesereau took the jury through the family's earlier accounts and the conflicts in them.

The scenes outside the courthouse in Santa Maria, with fans in sequins and news vans, were as much part of the story as the courtroom. A television camera was not allowed in the room, and the drawings, among them Robles's, became the pictures of the trial.

## Aftermath

The jury, which had eight women and four men, acquitted him on all fourteen counts in June 2005, including lesser charges. Jackson left California and lived abroad for most of the next three years.
