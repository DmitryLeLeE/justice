## The case

On the evening of 4 November 1995 Yitzhak Rabin, the prime minister of Israel, spoke at a rally in support of the peace process at Kings of Israel Square in Tel Aviv. As he went to his car, Yigal Amir, a 25-year-old law student, shot him twice with a Beretta pistol. Rabin died at Ichilov Hospital that night, and Amir was seized at once. He opposed the Oslo Accords, which gave Palestinians self-government in parts of the occupied territories, and said that a Jewish law of the pursuer, din rodef, justified killing a Jew who endangered others.

## In the courtroom

Amir confessed and made no attempt to deny the shooting. He was tried by three judges of the Tel Aviv-Jaffa District Court, Edmond Levy, Saviona Rotlevy and Oded Mudrich, who rejected his argument and on 27 March 1996 sentenced him to life imprisonment and to six years more for wounding Rabin's bodyguard. His brother Hagai, who had helped to obtain weapons, was sentenced to 12 years, increased to 16 on appeal, and their friend Dror Adani to seven.

## Aftermath

The Shamgar Commission, which investigated the security failure, reported on 28 March 1996 and criticised the Shin Bet for not protecting the prime minister adequately and for underestimating threats from Jewish extremists. Amir remains in prison. The assassination changed Israeli politics: Rabin's death ended the momentum of the peace process, and the memory of it is still contested.
