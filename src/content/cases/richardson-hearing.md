## The case

The Senate Judiciary Committee questioned Elliot Richardson on 22 May 1973 as Richard Nixon's nominee for Attorney General, at the height of the Watergate affair.

Richardson was confirmed. He resigned five months later in the Saturday Night Massacre.

## In the room

The Senate would not confirm a new Attorney General unless the nominee promised an independent investigation of Watergate. Richardson agreed to appoint a special prosecutor and to give him wide authority, and he named the Harvard professor Archibald Cox. He was confirmed by a large majority.

The drawing shows him at the witness table during the questioning.

## Aftermath

Five months later Nixon ordered Cox dismissed after Cox asked a court for the White House tapes. Richardson refused to carry out the order and resigned, as did his deputy William Ruckelshaus. Solicitor General Robert Bork then fired Cox. The public reaction forced Nixon to name a new special prosecutor.
