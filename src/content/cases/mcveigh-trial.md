## The case

At 9:02 on the morning of 19 April 1995 a truck bomb exploded outside the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City. 168 people died, including 19 children in a day-care centre, and more than 680 were injured. Within ninety minutes a state trooper, Charlie Hanger, stopped a car without a licence plate on an Oklahoma highway and arrested its driver, Timothy McVeigh, for carrying a concealed weapon. Investigators then linked him to the bombing.

## In the courtroom

Judge Richard Matsch ruled that a fair trial could not be held in Oklahoma, and moved it to Denver. Joseph Hartzler led a prosecution that traced the rented truck and the purchases of the explosives' ingredients to McVeigh and his friend Terry Nichols. Stephen Jones, for the defence, argued that others had been involved. On 2 June 1997 the jury found McVeigh guilty on all 11 counts and recommended death.

## Aftermath

McVeigh dropped his appeals and was executed on 11 June 2001, the first federal execution in 38 years. Nichols was convicted in a federal trial in 1997 of conspiracy and involuntary manslaughter and in a state trial in 2004 of 161 murders, and he is serving life terms without parole.

## 18+ · The victims

The dead ranged from infants to people in their seventies. They included staff of federal agencies and visitors to the building, and a rescue worker, Rebecca Anderson, who was struck by falling debris; most of the children were in the America's Kids day-care on the second floor. McVeigh described the deaths of the children as collateral damage in an interview before his execution, and the prosecution used his own words, spoken to friends before the bombing, to show that he had planned it carefully.
