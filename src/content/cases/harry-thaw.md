## The case

Stanford White was the best-known architect in New York and a man with a private life. Thaw, a rich and unstable young man from Pittsburgh, was married to the actress Evelyn Nesbit, who had told him that White had drugged and raped her when she was sixteen. On 25 June 1906, during a performance on the roof of Madison Square Garden, Thaw walked up to White's table and shot him three times in front of hundreds of people.

## In the courtroom

The first trial was called "the trial of the century" by the papers. It began in January 1907 and the defence argued that Thaw was insane, with the central testimony from Evelyn Nesbit herself. After forty-seven hours of deliberation the jury could not agree. At the second trial the defence, led by Martin Littleton, pleaded temporary insanity, and the jury accepted it.

## Aftermath

Thaw was committed to Matteawan, from which he escaped to Canada in 1913. He was returned in 1914, and in 1915 a jury decided he was no longer insane and he was released.
