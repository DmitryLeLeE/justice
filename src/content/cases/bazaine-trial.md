## The case

After the French defeat at Sedan, Bazaine and his army were shut up in Metz. When food ran out he surrendered the fortress and its men, and many in France called it treason. He returned after the war and demanded to be tried to clear his name.

## In the courtroom

The council of war met in the Trianon, with the Duc d'Aumale, a son of Louis-Philippe, presiding. The trial lasted two months and the prosecution called a great number of officers. Bazaine's defence was that he had done everything possible in an impossible situation and that the government of the day had abandoned him.

Hubert Clerget drew the courtroom for the press.

## Aftermath

The sentence was commuted and he was imprisoned on an island off Cannes, from which he escaped with the help of his wife and a cousin. He lived on in Spain and died in Madrid in 1888. Historians since have been kinder to him than his judges were, but the verdict stood.
