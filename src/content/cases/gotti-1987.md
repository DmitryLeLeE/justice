## The case

John Gotti, boss of the Gambino crime family, was tried in Brooklyn on racketeering charges in 1987.

The jury acquitted Gotti in March 1987. He was convicted in a second trial in 1992.

## In the courtroom

The prosecution used wiretaps, informers and an indictment that described the Gambino family as a criminal enterprise. Gotti sat in expensive suits, was cheerful in court and was seen by the press as a new kind of mob boss. His lawyer Bruce Cutler attacked the informants as career criminals.

The jury acquitted all the defendants. It later came out that one juror, George Pape, had been bribed and was convicted of it in 1990. Gotti had become the "Teflon Don".

## Aftermath

It took another five years and a different prosecutor, with the help of Gambino underboss Sammy "the Bull" Gravano, to convict him in 1992. He died in a federal prison hospital in 2002.
