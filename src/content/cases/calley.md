## The case

Lieutenant William Calley was court-martialled at Fort Benning for the 1968 massacre of civilians at My Lai in Vietnam.

On 31 March 1971 Calley was convicted of the premeditated murder of 22 civilians and sentenced to life. The sentence was later reduced and he was freed in 1974.

## In the courtroom

Howard Brodie drew the verdict day for CBS: Calley saluting the president of the six-officer jury. He was the only man convicted of the massacre. His defence was obedience to orders from Captain Ernest Medina, and Medina, tried next, was acquitted.

The prosecutor, Aubrey Daniel, believed the case was about murder and not about politics. Much of the American public disagreed: popular songs and petitions backed Calley, and President Nixon ordered him moved from the stockade to house arrest while his appeal was heard.

## Aftermath

The Army reduced his life term to twenty years and then ten. A federal judge freed him in 1974 on grounds of pretrial publicity. The Army fought the order, and he was released on parole that November. My Lai is still the best-known American war-crime prosecution of the Vietnam era, and for many people the sentence was too small for the crime.
