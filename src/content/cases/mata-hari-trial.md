## The case

Margaretha Zelle was born in the Netherlands and became famous in Paris before the war as the exotic dancer Mata Hari, with a long list of rich and powerful lovers. When the war began she travelled through Europe, and in 1916–17 the French counter-espionage service, which had been embarrassed by military failures, took an interest in her. German radio messages intercepted in January 1917 spoke of an agent code-named H-21. The French identified the agent as Zelle, though the code was one the Germans knew had been broken, which later scholars find suspicious. She was arrested on 13 February 1917 in her hotel on the Champs-Élysées.

## In the courtroom

Captain Pierre Bouchardon questioned her for months and prosecuted her at the trial on 24 July 1917 before a military court. Her lawyer, Édouard Clunet, a man in his seventies, was not allowed to cross-examine prosecution witnesses. She admitted that she took money from German officials but said that it was payment from lovers, and denied that she had ever spied. The charge was that she had caused the deaths of at least 50,000 soldiers. The court found her guilty.

## Aftermath

At dawn on 15 October 1917, Mata Hari was shot by a twelve-man firing squad in the moat of the Château de Vincennes. She was 41. Later biographers argue that she was a convenient scapegoat for France's military failures in 1917 and that the files contain falsifications; in 2017 the French army released 1,275 pages of the sealed records.
