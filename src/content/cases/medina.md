## The case

Captain Ernest Medina, Calley's company commander at My Lai, was court-martialled at Fort McPherson.

Medina was found not guilty in September 1971.

## In the courtroom

Medina was the company commander, and the Army's case was that he had ordered the killing of civilians or knew about it and let it go on. His defence, led by F. Lee Bailey, said that he had not known the scale of what happened and that he had called a halt as soon as he was aware. Calley, tried earlier, had said Medina gave the order; at Medina's own trial Calley changed his testimony.

Howard Brodie's drawing, made for CBS, shows the court.

## Aftermath

The jury of five officers acquitted him after a very short deliberation, and the court's instruction about command responsibility was seen as demanding. Medina later admitted that he had lied about the number of killings when the Army's investigators asked. Only Calley was convicted for My Lai.
