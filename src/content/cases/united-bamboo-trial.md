## The case

United Bamboo was a crime syndicate founded in Taiwan some thirty years earlier, with branches in Hong Kong, Japan, the Philippines, Singapore and American cities including Houston, Los Angeles, San Francisco and New York. The indictment, brought under the racketeering law, charged the leaders with a series of narcotics conspiracies and firearms offences, and with the October 1984 murder of the journalist Henry Liu in Daly City, California.

## In the courtroom

The trial before Judge Robert L. Carter in the Southern District of New York took eight weeks. The jury convicted each defendant on at least one count. Chang An-lo and Tung Kuei-Sen were acquitted of the racketeering counts, and others were convicted on one or two counts only. The longest term was twenty-five years.

Joseph Papin's drawing shows the courtroom with the judge presiding.

## Aftermath

The Second Circuit affirmed the convictions in 1988 and sent two sentences back for further findings. The court noted that the organization's proper name is United Bamboo, and not "Bamboo Union" as it is often called.
