## The case

On 11 July 1963 police raided Liliesleaf Farm outside Johannesburg, where leaders of Umkhonto we Sizwe, the armed wing of the African National Congress, were meeting. The court found the first indictment unsatisfactory; the second named ten accused. Mandela was already serving another sentence in a Pretoria prison and was added to the case.

The trial began in October 1963. Justice Quartus de Wet presided and Percy Yutar led the prosecution. The accused expected death sentences. On 20 April 1964 Mandela delivered a statement from the dock, and on 9 June the UN Security Council passed a resolution calling for an end to the trial.

The sentence was read on 12 June. Most of the convicted men were sent to Robben Island; Denis Goldberg, the only white man among them, was held in Pretoria Central Prison.

## In the courtroom

The state's case was built on documents seized at the farm and on witnesses. The defence, led by Bram Fischer, did not contest that its clients had planned sabotage, only that they had planned a guerrilla war. The accused had agreed to stress their political reasons.

Mandela opened the defence case on 20 April 1964 with a statement from the dock, about three hours long, that ended with the words that he was prepared to die for the ideal of a free and democratic society. The speech was printed abroad and became the best-known statement of the movement.

## Verdict and aftermath

The charges carried the death penalty. Judge de Wet convicted eight of the accused of sabotage and sentenced them to life imprisonment, a punishment widely seen as a response to the international protests. Bernstein was acquitted. Mandela was released in 1990 after 27 years in prison and became president of South Africa in 1994.
