## The case

On 15 April 2013 two pressure-cooker bombs exploded near the finish line of the Boston Marathon, killing three people, Krystle Campbell, Lü Lingzi and eight-year-old Martin Richard, and injuring more than 260. Three days later the brothers Tamerlan and Dzhokhar Tsarnaev shot and killed an MIT police officer, Sean Collier. The police tracked them to Watertown, where Tamerlan was killed in a gun battle. Dzhokhar was found wounded in a boat on a trailer in a back garden after a day-long lockdown of the city.

## In the courtroom

The trial before Judge George O'Toole opened in March 2015. The defence, led by Judy Clarke, who had also represented Kaczynski and Moussaoui, did not dispute that he had taken part. She said that the elder brother had been the leader and that Dzhokhar had followed him. The jury found him guilty of all 30 counts and, a month later, voted for death on six of them.

## Aftermath

In 2020 the First Circuit vacated the death sentences because the judge had not adequately examined jurors for bias from the heavy media coverage, but in March 2022 the Supreme Court reinstated them. Further appeals have been pursued since.
