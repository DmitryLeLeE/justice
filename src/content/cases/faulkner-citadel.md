## The case

Shannon Richey Faulkner had applied to the Citadel in 1993 with her sex left off the file. The college accepted her, then withdrew its offer when it found out. She sued, claiming that a state-supported college could not exclude her because she was a woman.

## In the courtroom

For two years she fought the school in court. Judge C. Weston Houck of the US District Court in Charleston ruled in her favour and allowed her to attend as a day student while the appeals ran. Pat Lopez drew the courtroom in 1994: Faulkner at the lower left with her lawyer Robert Black, the Citadel's attorneys in the background, a court reporter at the right and the judge at the back.

## Aftermath

In August 1995 she was admitted as a cadet and left after a week, citing harassment. In June 1996 the Supreme Court ruled in the VMI case that a state could not run an all-male military college, and the Citadel then admitted women. Today they make up about eight per cent of its cadets, according to the Library of Congress.
