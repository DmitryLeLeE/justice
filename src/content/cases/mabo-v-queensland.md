## The case

Eddie Koiki Mabo, a Meriam man from Mer (Murray Island) in the Torres Strait, learned while working at James Cook University in Townsville that the law treated his people's island as Crown land. In 1982 he and four other Meriam plaintiffs asked the High Court for a declaration that they held rights to the land of the Murray Islands under their own customs. The case would test the claim that Australia had been *terra nullius*, land belonging to no one, when the British arrived.

## In the courtroom

Queensland passed a law in 1985 meant to wipe out any such rights. In 1988, in what is called *Mabo (No 1)*, the High Court held by four votes to three that the law conflicted with the Racial Discrimination Act. The facts were then heard, and the High Court gave judgment in *Mabo (No 2)* on 3 June 1992. Six of the seven judges held that native title is part of Australian common law, resting on the traditional laws and customs of Indigenous people, and that the doctrine of terra nullius did not apply. Justice Dawson dissented. Native title could be extinguished by valid government action, but not by the mere fact of British settlement.

## Aftermath

Mabo died in January 1992 and did not live to see the judgment. The Keating government responded with the Native Title Act 1993, which set up a tribunal to hear claims, and the case became a turning point in debates about land and Indigenous rights in Australia. 3 June is now marked as Mabo Day.
