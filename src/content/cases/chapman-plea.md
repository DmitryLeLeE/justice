## The case

On 8 December 1980 John Lennon and Yoko Ono returned to the Dakota apartment building at West 72nd Street in Manhattan. Earlier that day Lennon had signed an album for the man waiting by the entrance, Mark David Chapman. Chapman shot him in the back as he walked into the archway. He then stayed at the scene, reading J. D. Salinger's "The Catcher in the Rye", until police arrived.

## In the courtroom

Chapman's lawyer, Jonathan Marks, entered a plea of not guilty by reason of insanity, and psychiatrists were retained. At a hearing in January 1981 Judge Dennis Edwards found him fit to stand trial. On 22 June 1981 Chapman announced that he wished to plead guilty, against the advice of Marks, saying that God had told him to. He spoke of the novel's hero, Holden Caulfield, as the model for his act, and at his sentencing on 24 August he read a passage from the book aloud to the court. The judge imposed 20 years to life with psychiatric treatment.

## Aftermath

Chapman has been refused parole at hearings every two years since 2000; the board has cited the risk to the public and the notoriety of the crime. The most recent refusal, the fourteenth, was on 27 August 2025.
