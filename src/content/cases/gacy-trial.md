## The case

John Wayne Gacy ran a building contractor's business in Norwood Park Township, on the north-west side of Chicago, and was active in local politics. From 1975 he also performed at parties and in children's hospitals as "Pogo the Clown". On 11 December 1978 a 15-year-old, Robert Piest, left a pharmacy in Des Plaines to talk to a contractor about a summer job, and did not come home. The pharmacy owner named Gacy. On 21 December police arrested him on a cannabis charge and began a search of his house.

## In the courtroom

In February 1980 Gacy was tried in Chicago for 33 murders. He admitted the killings to the police but his lawyers argued that he was legally insane, that he suffered from multiple personalities, and that he had killed without knowing what he did. Psychiatrists for the state disagreed, and testified that he had planned the crimes and tried to conceal them. The jury found him guilty and he was sentenced to death on 13 March 1980.

## Aftermath

Gacy spent fourteen years on death row, painting clowns and portraits and keeping up a correspondence. He was executed by lethal injection at Stateville on 10 May 1994. Several of his victims were not identified for decades; DNA work by Cook County has led to new identifications since 2011.

## 18+ · The crawl space

At least 33 young men and boys, aged from about 14 to 26, were killed between 1972 and 1978. Twenty-six were buried in the crawl space of Gacy's house, three elsewhere on the property and four were thrown into the Des Plaines River. Many of them had been lured to the house with offers of work or money. When police dug under the house on 21 December 1978 the remains were found in the soil of the crawl space.
