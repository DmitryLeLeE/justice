## The case

Abby Sage had married Daniel McFarland, a lawyer who drank and had a violent temper, and left him. She fell in love with Albert Richardson, a well-known war correspondent for the Tribune. In 1869 McFarland came into the Tribune building, found Richardson at his desk and shot him. Richardson lived for a week, and in that time Abby, whose divorce had come through in Indiana, married him at his bedside in a ceremony conducted by Beecher.

## In the courtroom

The defence did not deny the shooting. McFarland's lawyers argued that he was insane, driven mad by the loss of his wife and his child, and the trial became a hearing on Abby's morals. Historians describe the proceedings as a farce shaped by Tammany Hall connections and by hostility to the Tribune's staff. The jury acquitted him and the crowd cheered.

## Aftermath

Abby never went back to McFarland. She worked as a play reader for the theatre and died in 1900. McFarland went west.
