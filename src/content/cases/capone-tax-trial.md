## The case

Capone ran the Chicago Outfit during Prohibition and was linked to many killings, among them the St. Valentine's Day massacre of 1929, but witnesses disappeared and juries were bribed or frightened. Federal investigators pursued a different charge. They assembled evidence of what he had spent and what he had earned, although he paid no income tax, and in 1931 a grand jury indicted him on 22 counts covering the years 1925 to 1929.

## In the courtroom

Capone pleaded guilty in June under a deal that promised a short sentence. Judge Wilkerson refused to be bound by it, and in July the pleas were withdrawn and the case went to trial in the Chicago Federal Building in October. The government proved lavish spending through witnesses who had sold him cars, jewellery and furnishings. On 17 October the jury convicted him of five counts. A week later Wilkerson sentenced him to eleven years in prison, a $50,000 fine and court costs, and ordered him to pay back taxes.

## Aftermath

Capone entered the federal penitentiary in Atlanta in 1932 and was moved to Alcatraz in 1934. He was paroled in November 1939 with his health damaged by syphilis, and he died at his Florida home in 1947.
