## The case

In September 1922 the Greek army was defeated in Asia Minor, and a military revolution brought down the government. The new authorities arrested several former ministers and the commander-in-chief.

An extraordinary court martial tried them in Athens in November 1922 for high treason. Six men were condemned and executed within hours of the verdict. The sketch used here shows the president of the court, Alexandros Othonaios, and is a photograph of a drawing shown in a Benaki Museum exhibition on Asia Minor.

## In the courtroom

The six were made responsible for the defeat in Asia Minor. They included the last prime minister, Gounaris, and the commander-in-chief, Hatzianestis. The trial lasted only a few days, and the defendants had little time to prepare. Foreign governments protested, and the executions went ahead within hours of the verdict.

## Aftermath

The shooting of the six divided Greek politics for a generation, and historians still argue about whether the trial was justice or revenge for the defeat.
