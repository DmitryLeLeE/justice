## The case

John Hinckley was tried in Washington for shooting President Reagan and three other men in March 1981. The drawing dates from 12 May 1982.

On 21 June 1982 the jury found Hinckley not guilty by reason of insanity. He was committed to a psychiatric hospital.

## In the courtroom

Hinckley had shot the president, a press secretary, a policeman and a Secret Service agent in front of television cameras. No one disputed that he had pulled the trigger. The fight was over his mind: defence psychiatrists said he was schizophrenic and obsessed with the actress Jodie Foster and with the film Taxi Driver; the prosecution said he was calculating and had planned the attack.

Under the federal rules then in force the prosecution had to prove that he was sane. It could not convince the jury.

## Aftermath

The public reaction was fierce. Congress and a number of states changed the law, moved the burden of proof to the defendant and in some places abolished the insanity defence. Hinckley was held at St. Elizabeths Hospital, gradually given leave to live with his mother, and was released from all court conditions in June 2022.
