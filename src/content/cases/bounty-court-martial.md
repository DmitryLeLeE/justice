## The case

On 28 April 1789 the crew of HMS *Bounty*, led by Fletcher Christian, seized the ship from Lieutenant William Bligh. Bligh and eighteen men were cast adrift in the ship's launch and reached Timor after a voyage of some 3,600 nautical miles. Some of the mutineers stayed on Tahiti, and the Royal Navy sent HMS *Pandora* to bring them back. Fourteen men were arrested and held in a cage on the deck, and when *Pandora* was wrecked on the Great Barrier Reef in 1791 several of the prisoners drowned. Ten reached England.

## In the courtroom

The court martial opened on 12 September 1792 on board HMS *Duke* in Portsmouth Harbour, with Vice-Admiral Lord Hood presiding. Four men were acquitted. On 18 September six were found guilty of mutiny and sentenced to hang, with the court recommending mercy for Peter Heywood, a midshipman, and for James Morrison. Heywood had come from a well-connected family from the Isle of Man, and his family worked to secure his pardon.

## Aftermath

Heywood and Morrison were pardoned by the king on 26 October 1792, and William Muspratt later won a respite and a pardon. The other three were hanged from the yardarm of HMS *Brunswick* in Portsmouth in October 1792. Heywood went back to the navy and became a captain. Christian and the mutineers who had fled to Pitcairn Island were never tried.
