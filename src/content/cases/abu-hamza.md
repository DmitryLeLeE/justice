## The case

The former imam of the Finsbury Park mosque in London, extradited from Britain, was tried in a New York federal court on terrorism charges.

Abu Hamza was convicted in May 2014 and sentenced to life imprisonment in January 2015.

## In the courtroom

The man in the drawing had been an imam at the Finsbury Park Mosque in north London. He had lost both hands, and in court wore prosthetic arms; courtroom artist Elizabeth Williams drew them carefully and drew his stiff manner with the defence paralegal beside him.

The prosecution built its case on the 1998 kidnapping in Yemen, on a plot to set up a terrorist training camp at a ranch in Oregon, and on support for a camp in Afghanistan. Witnesses included survivors of the Yemen hostage-taking. Abu Hamza testified in his own defence and denied any operational role.

## Aftermath

The jury convicted him on all eleven counts. He is held in a high-security federal prison. The case showed how a London preacher could be tried in New York, on charges that reached back more than fifteen years.
