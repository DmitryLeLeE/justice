## The case

In the early hours of 25 November 2006, plainclothes New York police detectives fired dozens of shots at a car outside a Queens nightclub, killing Sean Bell, who was due to be married later that day. Two of his friends were wounded.

Three detectives were tried in Queens in the spring of 2008 on charges including manslaughter, before a judge without a jury. The courtroom artist Elizabeth Williams covered the trial, and her sketch of the cross-examination of a witness is shown here.

## In the courtroom

The detectives had been on an undercover operation at the club. They said they believed Bell and his friends had a gun, and that one of them, Isnora, heard a threat. No gun was found. The defendants chose to be tried by a judge alone.

The judge said that the prosecution had not proved its case beyond a reasonable doubt, and that the detectives' accounts could not be dismissed because of some inconsistencies in the testimony of the witnesses for the state.

## Aftermath

The verdict brought demonstrations in the city. The federal government declined to bring civil-rights charges in 2010, and the city settled with Bell's family the same year.
