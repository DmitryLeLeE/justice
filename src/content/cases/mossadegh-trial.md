## The case

Mohammad Mossadegh became prime minister of Iran in 1951 and nationalised the oil industry, which had been run by the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company. Britain and the United States helped to organise the coup that removed him in August 1953 and returned the Shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, to full power. Mossadegh's aides hid him while a mob attacked his house, and the next day he surrendered at the Officers' Club, where General Fazlollah Zahedi, the new prime minister, had set up his headquarters.

## In the courtroom

He was tried by a military tribunal in Tehran, in a hearing that opened in November 1953. Mossadegh, then 71, spoke for himself at length and challenged the authority of the court, and the hearings were reported around the world. The prosecutors asked for the death penalty. On 21 December 1953 the tribunal sentenced him to three years in solitary confinement in a military prison, and he is said to have remarked that the verdict had added to his place in history.

## Aftermath

When his sentence ended in 1956 Mossadegh was not freed but confined to his estate at Ahmadabad, west of Tehran, where he stayed under house arrest until he died of cancer on 5 March 1967. In Iran he came to be seen as a symbol of resistance to foreign control of the country's resources, and in 2013 the CIA publicly acknowledged its part in the coup.
