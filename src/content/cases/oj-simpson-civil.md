## The case

Families of the two people killed in 1994 sued O. J. Simpson for wrongful death in Santa Monica, after his acquittal in the criminal trial. The drawing dates from 6 December 1996.

The jury found Simpson liable in February 1997 and awarded $33.5 million in damages.

## In the courtroom

The civil case needed the plaintiffs to show only that it was more likely than not that Simpson had caused the deaths. Unlike the criminal trial, it was not televised, and Simpson was called to testify by the plaintiffs, who confronted him with photographs of him wearing the type of shoes that left the bloody footprints.

The jury was drawn from Santa Monica, a more mixed pool than the downtown Los Angeles panel in 1995. It found him liable for both deaths, then awarded $8.5 million in compensatory damages and $25 million as punishment.

## Aftermath

Simpson paid little of the judgment, which grew with interest. In 2007 he was arrested in Las Vegas in an unrelated armed robbery and was sentenced to 33 years. He was paroled in 2017.
