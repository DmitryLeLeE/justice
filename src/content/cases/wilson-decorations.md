## The case

In 1887 an investigation into a trade in honours exposed Daniel Wilson, a deputy and the son-in-law of President Jules Grévy, who was accused of using his access to the Élysée to sell decorations. The scandal led to Grévy's resignation in December 1887.

Wilson was tried before the criminal court in February 1888. The Cri du peuple printed a sketch of the first hearing. He was convicted at first instance and sentenced to two years and a fine, but the Court of Appeal quashed the conviction.

## In the courtroom

The trade was run from the Élysée Palace itself, through Wilson's connections. The honour was said to cost its buyers tens of thousands of francs. The affair happened at a time when the Third Republic was shaken by General Boulanger, and it fed the view that republican politicians were corrupt.

## Aftermath

Wilson was sentenced to two years in prison and a fine, but on appeal the court decided that the law on fraud did not cover his actions, and he went free. He was re-elected to the Chamber the next year.
