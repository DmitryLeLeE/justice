In 1887 an investigation into a trade in honours exposed Daniel Wilson, a deputy and the son-in-law of President Jules Grévy, who was accused of using his access to the Élysée to sell decorations. The scandal led to Grévy's resignation in December 1887.

Wilson was tried before the criminal court in February 1888. The Cri du peuple printed a sketch of the first hearing. He was convicted at first instance and sentenced to two years and a fine, but the Court of Appeal quashed the conviction.
