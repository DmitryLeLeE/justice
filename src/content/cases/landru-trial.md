## The case

Between 1915 and 1919 Landru placed advertisements for marriage and took in women who had lost their husbands in the war. Ten women and a young man, the son of one of them, vanished after going to visit him at his villas outside Paris, the last of them at Gambais. The police found that he had sold their furniture and cashed their cheques. They found no bodies, only bone fragments in a kitchen stove, and a notebook in which he had recorded the names and the rail fares.

## In the courtroom

The trial at Versailles in the autumn of 1921 was one of the great spectacles of the time, with a public that included famous writers and actors. Landru, small and bald with a long black beard, sat calmly and answered the charges with irony. His counsel, Vincent de Moro-Giafferri, argued that without a body there could be no murder. The court did not accept it.

The drawing shows Inspector Belin, who had led the investigation, giving evidence.

## Aftermath

He was guillotined in the morning of 25 February 1922 at Versailles. He never admitted anything. Chaplin's film Monsieur Verdoux, in 1947, was suggested by his story.
