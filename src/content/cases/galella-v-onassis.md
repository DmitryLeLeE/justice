## The case

Jacqueline Onassis sued the photographer Ron Galella for harassment. Judge Irving Ben Cooper heard the case.

The court ordered Galella to keep his distance from Onassis and her children.

## In the courtroom

Galella made a living photographing public figures, and his trademark was getting close, in the street, at her children's schools and on the water. Onassis said he had ruined her privacy and frightened her children. Galella said he was a journalist and had a right to take pictures in public. Judge Cooper sided with her, and a long-lens court fight began.

The drawing dates from the 1982 hearing, when Onassis went back to court because he had once again got too near. Marilyn Church's drawing of Onassis in court dates from that hearing.

## Aftermath

The Second Circuit had shaved the original restraining order to 25 feet. In 1982 Galella agreed to give up photographing her altogether. The case is cited in discussions of privacy and press freedom, and Galella's own pictures of Onassis are now widely exhibited.
