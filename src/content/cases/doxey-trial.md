## The case

Dora Doxey had married William Erder in April 1909 while she was still married to the physician Loren Doxey. Erder died in July. Five months later St. Louis authorities found arsenic in his body, and a grand jury indicted both Doxeys for murder.

## In the courtroom

Charles P. Johnson, a former lieutenant governor of Missouri, defended both of them. The murder charges were separated, and Dora was tried in May and June 1910. Many of the spectators were women, and Marguerite Martyn, an artist on the Post-Dispatch, sketched them. The jury acquitted Dora on 3 June.

## Aftermath

Dora was tried separately for bigamy, and after years of postponements she pleaded guilty and was jailed in December 1913. She was released in February 1914 and went to Idaho. Loren drowned in the Tennessee River in 1912, and a coroner's jury ruled it an accident. Dora died in San Francisco in 1921.
