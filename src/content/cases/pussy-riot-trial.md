## The case

On 21 February 2012 five members of the group Pussy Riot, in balaclavas, ran in front of the altar of the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow and performed a "punk prayer" calling on the Virgin Mary to drive out Vladimir Putin, a few weeks before Putin was elected to a third term as president. Security guards removed them after less than a minute. In March the police arrested Tolokonnikova, Alyokhina and Samutsevich, and held them in pre-trial detention for five months.

## In the courtroom

The trial, which began on 30 July 2012 at the Khamovniki court before Judge Marina Syrova, was held in a glass cage in the courtroom. The prosecution said that the women had acted from hatred of the Orthodox faith, and the three defended themselves by saying that their protest was political and that they had meant no offence to believers. On 17 August the judge found them guilty of hooliganism motivated by religious hatred and sentenced each to two years in a penal colony.

## Aftermath

Samutsevich's sentence was suspended in October 2012 on the ground that she had been stopped by the guards before she could take part. Alyokhina and Tolokonnikova served most of their terms in penal colonies and were freed in December 2013 under an amnesty, a few weeks before the Sochi Winter Olympics. In 2018 the European Court of Human Rights found that Russia had violated their rights.
