## The case

On 19 April 1995 Timothy McVeigh detonated a rental truck filled with explosives outside the federal building in Oklahoma City, killing 168 people. It was the second most deadly terrorist attack on the United States up to that time. Judge Richard Matsch of Denver was named to hear the case in place of Judge Wayne Alley, whose chambers and courtroom had been destroyed in the blast.

## In the hearing

Defence lawyers asked for the trial to be moved on the ground that publicity and local feeling would make a fair jury impossible. Dr. Steven Penrod, an expert on eyewitness behaviour, testified on 1 February 1996, with McVeigh and Nichols sitting at the defence table. Pat Lopez drew the scene.

## Aftermath

On 20 February Matsch ordered the trial moved to Denver. McVeigh was convicted and sentenced to death in 1997 and executed by lethal injection on 11 June 2001. Nichols was convicted of conspiracy and manslaughter in federal court and later in state court, and is serving consecutive life sentences at the federal prison in Florence, Colorado.
