## The case

Barbie ran the Gestapo in Lyon from 1942, and is held responsible for the arrest and torture of the Resistance leader Jean Moulin and for the roundup of forty-four Jewish children from the home at Izieu in 1944. After the war the American intelligence service protected him, and he lived in Bolivia under a false name until 1983. France tried him not for war crimes, which were time-barred, but for crimes against humanity, which are not.

## In the courtroom

The trial was filmed for the archives, a first for a French court. Barbie asked to be left out of most of the hearings and was absent from the courtroom for much of the trial. His lawyer, Jacques Vergès, who had defended Algerian nationalists, tried to widen the case to France's own record of colonial violence, and the survivors who testified and the civil parties' lawyers made the court a place for the history of the Occupation.

Calvi's drawing shows Vergès beside his client.

## Aftermath

Barbie was sentenced to life and died of cancer in the prison of Saint-Joseph in Lyon. The trial opened the way for the trials of Paul Touvier and Maurice Papon.
