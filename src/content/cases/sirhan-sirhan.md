## The case

Sirhan Sirhan was tried in Los Angeles for the shooting of Senator Robert F. Kennedy at the Ambassador Hotel in June 1968.

Sirhan was convicted of first-degree murder in April 1969 and sentenced to death. The sentence was commuted to life imprisonment in 1972.

## In the courtroom

The defence did not dispute that Sirhan fired the shots. Cooper's strategy was to argue diminished capacity, so that the charge would be reduced from first-degree murder to a lesser one, and psychiatrists described Sirhan as a man with a disturbed mind. Sirhan himself at one stage asked to plead guilty, and the court did not accept it.

## Aftermath

Sirhan has been refused parole many times. Some researchers have questioned the ballistic evidence over the years, but the conviction has stood. Arnold Mesches drew him during the proceedings.
