## The case

Bal Gangadhar Tilak edited the Marathi newspaper *Kesari* in Pune and was among the most prominent Indian nationalists of his day. In 1897 plague swept western India and the government's campaign against it, which included house searches, was widely resented. On 22 June 1897 the Chapekar brothers shot the plague commissioner Walter Rand and another officer, Lt. Ayerst, in Pune. The authorities charged Tilak over articles in the *Kesari*, saying that they stirred up hatred of the government.

## In the courtroom

The first trial took place in the Bombay High Court in 1897 and ended in a sentence of 18 months in prison. The second came after the bomb attack at Muzaffarpur on 30 April 1908, which Tilak's articles in the *Kesari* defended as the work of desperate men. He was tried in Bombay before Justice Dinshaw Davar, who had himself appeared for Tilak in 1897. Muhammad Ali Jinnah, then a young barrister, defended him. A special jury convicted him by seven votes to two, and Davar described the articles as seething with sedition.

## Aftermath

Tilak was sent to Mandalay in Burma for six years, and released on 16 June 1914. He then became a leader of the Home Rule movement, and his saying that self-rule was his birthright was taken up by the nationalist cause. He died in 1920.
