## The case

Milošević led Serbia from 1989 and Yugoslavia from 1997, and was in power through the wars that broke up Yugoslavia in Croatia, Bosnia and Kosovo. The Tribunal indicted him in May 1999, during the NATO bombing of Yugoslavia, for crimes in Kosovo, and later added charges for Croatia and Bosnia, including genocide. After he lost power in October 2000, the Serbian government of Zoran Đinđić handed him over to The Hague in June 2001.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened in February 2002. Milošević refused to recognise the Tribunal, dismissed court-appointed lawyers and defended himself, cross-examining prosecution witnesses at length and turning the hearings into political speeches. The prosecution case, presented by Carla Del Ponte and Geoffrey Nice, took more than two years and 295 witnesses. His blood pressure and heart disease caused many delays, and when presiding judge Richard May resigned for health reasons in 2004, Patrick Robinson took over. The Tribunal imposed defence counsel on him in 2004, and the defence case was still in progress when he died.

## Aftermath

Milošević was found dead in his cell at the detention unit in Scheveningen on 11 March 2006. The Tribunal said that he had died of a heart attack, and it found no evidence of poisoning, although he had been taking medicines in a way that his doctors had warned against. Without a verdict, many victims felt that justice was incomplete.

## 18+ · What the indictments covered

The indictments covered the killing of civilians in Croatia, such as at Vukovar, the camps, shelling and mass killings in Bosnia, including Srebrenica, and the expulsion of hundreds of thousands of Kosovo Albanians in 1999. Witnesses described the shooting of civilians, rape and detention in camps. The prosecution said that Milošević directed or supported the forces responsible, and he denied all the charges.
