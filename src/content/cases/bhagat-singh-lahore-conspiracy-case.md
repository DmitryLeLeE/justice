## The case

On 17 December 1928 members of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association shot John Saunders, an assistant superintendent of police in Lahore, whom they mistook for the officer they were after, James Scott. On 8 April 1929 Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw two low-intensity bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi and gave themselves up. On 12 June 1929 they were sentenced to life imprisonment for that. The police then linked Singh to the Saunders killing.

## In the courtroom

The Lahore Conspiracy Case opened on 10 July 1929 at Borstal Jail in Lahore. Singh and his comrades turned to a hunger strike for the rights of political prisoners; it lasted 116 days for Singh and ended on 5 October 1929, and one of the strikers, Jatin Das, died after 63 days. In May 1930 the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, issued an ordinance setting up a special tribunal of three High Court judges, with appeals only to the Privy Council in London. The accused boycotted the court, so the case went on largely without them. On 7 October 1930 the tribunal sentenced Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru to death.

## Aftermath

The Privy Council rejected the appeal. The three men were hanged in Lahore jail on the evening of 23 March 1931, a day earlier than scheduled; Singh was 23. His death made him one of the best-known figures of the independence movement, and his slogan "Inquilab Zindabad" ("Long live the revolution") is still used in India and Pakistan.
