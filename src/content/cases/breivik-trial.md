## The case

On 22 July 2011 Breivik set off a car bomb in the government quarter of Oslo at 15:25, killing eight people, and then drove to Utøya, an island in Tyrifjorden where the Labour Party's youth league was holding its summer camp. Dressed as a police officer, he shot at the participants for more than an hour, until 18:34, when armed police arrived and he surrendered. In all 77 people died and more than 300 were injured. He admitted the acts and said that they were justified as a defence of Europe against Islam and multiculturalism.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened on 16 April 2012 in Oslo. Everything depended on the court's finding on his sanity. Two psychiatric evaluations disagreed: the first found paranoid schizophrenia, and the second found no psychosis, but antisocial and narcissistic personality disorders. Breivik himself insisted that he was sane and said that being declared insane would be worse than death. Lippestad, the defence lawyer, said at the end that Breivik's conduct was the product of an inability to cope with reality; the prosecutors in the end asked that he be committed to psychiatric care, which was an unusual position, while Breivik wanted a prison sentence.

The court found him sane and, on 24 August 2012, sentenced him to 21 years of preventive detention with a ten-year minimum. The term can be prolonged for as long as he is judged dangerous. Neither side appealed.

## Aftermath

In 2016 the Oslo District Court held that his isolation in prison breached the ban on inhuman treatment, and an appeal court overturned the decision in 2017; the Supreme Court declined to take the case. A new court again rejected his complaint in 2024. Norway has no death penalty and no life sentence in the ordinary sense, and the sentence became a standing example in debates about how a state should answer a mass killing.

## 18+ · Utøya

Most of the 69 people killed on the island were teenagers. Breivik walked across it shooting people at close range, called others out of hiding by pretending to be a police officer there to help them, and shot at those who swam away from the shore. The 77 dead and the 323 injured include those hurt by the bomb in Oslo. The proceedings included the prosecution's reading out of the names, ages and wounds of the victims, and testimony from survivors.
