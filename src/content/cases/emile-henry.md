## The case

On 12 February 1894 Émile Henry threw a bomb into the Café Terminus near the Gare Saint-Lazare in Paris, killing one person and wounding many others. He was arrested at the scene.

His trial before the Seine Assize Court opened in late April 1894. Henry did not deny the attack and gave a long statement presenting it as a political act. The press covered the hearing with sketches, among them the one from Le Matin shown here. He was condemned to death and executed less than a month later.

## In the courtroom

Henry was a young, educated man, a former science student, and he made his own defence. He said the Café Terminus bomb was revenge for the execution of Vaillant and for the state's treatment of anarchists, and that the people in the café were not innocent because they were content with the order that harmed the poor. He did not ask for mercy.

Le Matin published a drawing of Henry beside his lawyer, Hornbostel. L'Univers illustré, in May, printed a portrait of his mother, Rose Caubert, who was present at the trial.

## Aftermath

He was sentenced to death and did not ask for mercy. A month after his execution an anarchist stabbed President Sadi Carnot to death in Lyon, and in July the Chamber passed the last of the "lois scélérates", the laws that criminalised anarchist propaganda. Henry's speech in court was widely read, and not only by anarchists.
