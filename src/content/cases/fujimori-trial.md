## The case

Alberto Fujimori was president of Peru from 1990 to 2000. His government fought the Shining Path and other guerrillas, and in 1992 he closed Congress and ruled with the army. After a bribery scandal he fled to Japan in 2000 and sent in his resignation by fax from Tokyo on 17 November; Congress refused to accept it and removed him. He later travelled to Chile, where he was arrested in November 2005 at Peru's request, and was extradited to Lima in September 2007.

## In the courtroom

The trial before three judges of the Supreme Court's special criminal chamber lasted from December 2007 until the spring of 2009, and was televised. The prosecution said that Fujimori had approved the actions of the Colina group, a death squad of army intelligence officers, which killed fifteen people at a party in the Barrios Altos district of Lima in November 1991 and nine students and a professor from the La Cantuta university in July 1992. It also charged two kidnappings. Fujimori said that he had not ordered any killings. On 7 April 2009 the court sentenced him to 25 years.

## Aftermath

It was the first time that a democratically elected head of state had been extradited to his own country, tried there and convicted of human rights abuses. President Pedro Pablo Kuczynski pardoned Fujimori on 24 December 2017, on the ground of his health. The Supreme Court annulled the pardon in 2018, and the Constitutional Court restored it in 2022. Fujimori was released in December 2023 and died on 11 September 2024.
