## The case

General Efraín Ríos Montt ruled Guatemala from March 1982 to August 1983, during the most violent period of the country's 36-year civil war. A truth commission later concluded that the army had carried out acts of genocide against the Maya population, and that Ríos Montt's government destroyed hundreds of villages. After he left office he remained in public life and became president of Congress; his parliamentary immunity ended in 2012 and he was charged.

## In the courtroom

The trial opened in Guatemala City in March 2013 before a panel led by Judge Yassmin Barrios. It heard the testimony of more than 100 witnesses, among them Maya Ixil survivors, who described what had been done to them and their families. The court found that the army had killed 1,771 Ixil people, and on 10 May 2013 convicted Ríos Montt of genocide and crimes against humanity, giving him 80 years: 50 for genocide and 30 for crimes against humanity. His co-defendant, the military intelligence chief José Mauricio Rodríguez Sánchez, was acquitted.

## Aftermath

On 20 May 2013 the Constitutional Court annulled the proceedings on technical grounds, in a decision that was widely criticised. A new trial began in 2015, but the court found that Ríos Montt, who had dementia, was not fit to take part, and the hearings were held behind closed doors. He died in April 2018 without a final verdict.

## 18+ · The testimony

Witnesses told the court that soldiers had burned houses with people inside, killed children and the old, and raped women and girls. A large part of the evidence came from forensic anthropologists who had exhumed mass graves, and from survivors who spoke in their own language through interpreters. Several women testified to sexual violence by soldiers.
