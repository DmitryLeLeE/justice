## The case

Car maker John DeLorean was tried in Los Angeles for conspiring to traffic cocaine, after an FBI sting recorded him. The drawing shows the artists themselves at work in court.

DeLorean was acquitted on 16 August 1984, on the ground of entrapment.

## In the courtroom

The government's case rested on videotapes in which DeLorean, whose car company was close to collapse, was shown a suitcase of cocaine and called it "better than gold". The defence did not deny the tapes. It argued that an FBI informant, a convicted drug dealer named James Hoffman, had led him to it. The jury agreed that the government had gone too far.

## Aftermath

DeLorean walked free, but his car company had already gone under in 1982, and he died in 2005. Elizabeth Williams, whose drawing of the courtroom artists at work is in the Library of Congress, was one of those covering the trial.
