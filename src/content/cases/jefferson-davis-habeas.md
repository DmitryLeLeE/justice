## The case

Davis was captured in Georgia in May 1865 and held at Fort Monroe, Virginia, without trial, partly in irons. The government hesitated: a treason trial in Virginia risked a Southern jury and an argument that secession was lawful,. His lawyers obtained a writ of habeas corpus.

## In the courtroom

On 13 May 1867, General Burton delivered him to the court at Richmond. The hearing was brief. A bond of $100,000 was set, and prominent Northerners signed it, among them the editor Horace Greeley and the financier Cornelius Vanderbilt. Davis walked free.

The illustration shown here was drawn by a special artist for Frank Leslie's Illustrated Newspaper.

## Aftermath

The case was put off again and again. President Johnson's Christmas amnesty of 1868 covered former Confederates, and in February 1869 the government dropped the case. Davis lived until 1889 and never regained his citizenship.
