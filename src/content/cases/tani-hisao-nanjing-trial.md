## The case

In December 1937 the Japanese army captured Nanjing, then the capital of China. Over the following weeks its soldiers killed large numbers of civilians and disarmed Chinese soldiers, and raped many women. The 6th Division, under Lieutenant General Tani Hisao, was among the units that took part in the battle for the city. After Japan's surrender, American occupation authorities arrested Tani in February 1946 on war crimes charges, and China asked for his extradition.

## In the courtroom

Tani was sent to Nanjing in August 1946 and tried before the Nanjing War Crimes Tribunal, which sat in the city where the crimes had been committed. The court heard the testimony of survivors who came to describe what they had seen. Tani denied all the charges. The tribunal ruled that Japanese commanders shared responsibility for the atrocities and sentenced him to death.

## Aftermath

On 26 April 1947 Tani was shot in public at Mount Yuhuatai on the edge of the city, where large numbers of Chinese had been killed ten years earlier. The tribunal's findings, and those of the international tribunal in Tokyo, which sentenced General Iwane Matsui, the commander of the force, to death, are the foundation of the historical record of the massacre.
