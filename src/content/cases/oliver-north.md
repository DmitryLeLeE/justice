## The case

Lieutenant Colonel Oliver North was tried in Washington over his part in the Iran-Contra affair. Judge Gerhard Gesell presided.

North was convicted on three counts in May 1989. The convictions were later vacated on appeal and the charges dismissed.

## In the courtroom

The case concerned the secret sale of arms to Iran and the diversion of profits to the Contra rebels in Nicaragua at a time when Congress had banned military aid. North, a Marine officer on the National Security Council staff, had become a national figure during his televised testimony to Congress in 1987.

The trial was delayed and cut back because the government would not release some classified material. North was acquitted of nine counts and convicted of three: helping to obstruct Congress, destroying documents, and accepting an illegal gratuity (a security fence at his home).

## Aftermath

Judge Gesell gave him a suspended sentence, probation and a fine. The appeals court sent the case back because his immunised testimony to Congress might have shaped the testimony of trial witnesses, and in 1991 the prosecutors dropped the charges rather than try to prove otherwise. President George H. W. Bush pardoned six other Iran-Contra figures in December 1992.
