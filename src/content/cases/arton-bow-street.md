## The case

The collapse of the French Panama Canal company in 1889 led to a long political scandal over bribes paid to deputies and officials. Émile Arton, who had acted as an intermediary, left France to avoid prosecution.

He was arrested in London in 1895 and appeared on 21 November at Bow Street, where an artist for a French paper sketched the hearing.

## Why he mattered

Arton had been the go-between of the Panama Canal Company, handing out money and shares to deputies and journalists. When the scandal broke in 1892 he vanished, and for three years French politicians had every reason to hope he stayed away: a man who knew the names could name them.

## At Bow Street

The hearing was brief and legal in its terms. The French request cited a swindle involving a dynamite company, not the bribes, and the magistrate found it sufficient. The drawing shows Arton in the dock in a London police court, a long way from the Chamber of Deputies where his old clients sat.

## Aftermath

Once back in France he was tried, sentenced to six years of hard labour, and pardoned in 1899. The Panama names he might have given were never publicly aired in court, and many readers took that as the real verdict.
