## The case

Elma Sands, a young woman who lived at a boarding house in lower Manhattan, vanished on the evening of 22 December 1799. She had told others she was going to meet her suitor, Levi Weeks, a carpenter who lodged in the same house. Her body was found in the Manhattan Well on 2 January 1800. Weeks, who was a brother of a wealthy builder, was arrested.

## In the courtroom

Weeks's brother Ezra hired three of the best lawyers in New York, among them the great rivals Alexander Hamilton and Aaron Burr. The trial lasted only two days. The prosecution relied on circumstantial evidence, and the defence attacked its witnesses. The jury was out for five minutes.

The picture is a wood engraving published in 1882, long after the trial, and shows an incident attributed to Burr. It is not a contemporary courtroom drawing, and is included for that reason with this note.

## Aftermath

The public believed Weeks was guilty, and he left the city. He moved to Natchez, Mississippi, became a respected architect and builder, and died there in 1819. Four years after the trial Burr shot Hamilton in a duel.
