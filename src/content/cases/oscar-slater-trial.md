## The case

Marion Gilchrist, an elderly woman, was found beaten to death in her flat in Glasgow on 21 December 1908. A brooch was missing. Witnesses described a man seen running from the building, and the police concentrated on Oscar Slater, a German-Jewish immigrant who had left for New York a few days after the murder, and who had pawned a brooch before leaving. He sailed under his own name, and returned voluntarily to face trial in Scotland. The brooch he had pawned turned out to be a different one.

## In the courtroom

At the trial in Edinburgh in May 1909 several witnesses identified Slater after being shown his photograph or seeing him before the identification, and the prosecution suggested that his departure showed guilt. The judge's summing up was hostile to Slater. By a majority the jury found the charge proved, and he was sentenced to death. Two days before the date of the execution the Secretary for Scotland commuted the sentence to life.

## Aftermath

Doubts grew. A police detective, John Thomson Trench, said that a witness had first named someone else, and was dismissed from the force; Arthur Conan Doyle published a pamphlet in 1912 attacking the evidence. Slater was freed in November 1927 after more evidence emerged, and in July 1928 the Court of Criminal Appeal quashed his conviction. He received a payment of £6,000.
