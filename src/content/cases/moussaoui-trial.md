## The case

In August 2001 flight-school instructors in Minnesota reported a man who wanted to learn to fly a large jet without any interest in taking off or landing. Zacarias Moussaoui was arrested by immigration agents on 16 August. After the attacks of 11 September the government charged him as a conspirator, though he had been in custody when they happened.

## In the courtroom

Moussaoui first insisted on defending himself, filed bizarre motions and denounced the court and his lawyers. In 2005 he pleaded guilty to all six counts, and said that he had planned to fly a plane into the White House as part of a later attack. The only issue left was whether he should be put to death. At the sentencing trial in 2006 before Judge Leonie Brinkema, the jury heard from relatives of those killed on 11 September; the defence argued that his role was minor and that he was mentally ill. The jury was unable to agree unanimously on death, so the sentence was life without parole.

## Aftermath

Moussaoui was moved to the federal supermax prison in Florence, Colorado, where he remains. He later tried to withdraw his guilty plea, but the courts refused.
