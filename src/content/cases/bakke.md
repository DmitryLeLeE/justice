## The case

The Supreme Court heard argument on 12 October 1977 on whether a medical school could reserve places for minority applicants.

On 28 June 1978 the Court barred strict racial quotas but allowed race to be considered as one factor in admissions.

## In the courtroom

Marilyn Church drew Archibald Cox, the former Watergate special prosecutor, at the lectern on the opening day. The case was unusual even for the Supreme Court: dozens of friend-of-the-court briefs, a packed gallery and a Court that was deeply split.

Davis had set aside sixteen of one hundred places in each entering class for minority applicants. Bakke, a white former engineer and Marine officer, had higher scores than some of those admitted.

## The decision

No single opinion commanded a majority. Justice Lewis Powell sided with four justices to strike down the fixed quota and with the other four to say that race could still be one factor in admissions. Bakke was admitted. For decades afterwards, universities designed their programmes around Powell's controlling opinion, until the Supreme Court ended race-conscious admissions in 2023.
