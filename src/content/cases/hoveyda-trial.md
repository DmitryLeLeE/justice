## The case

Amir-Abbas Hoveyda was prime minister of Iran from January 1965 until August 1977, the longest term in the country's history, and later served as court minister. He was detained on 7 November 1978 with about 60 other former officials in the Shah's last months, and held in comfortable house arrest. When the Shah left Iran, the guards at the place where Hoveyda was held abandoned their posts, but he refused to flee and handed himself over to the new authorities.

## In the courtroom

The revolutionary courts were set up in the weeks after the revolution, and tried members of the old regime with little or no defence, often in a single sitting. Hoveyda first appeared before the Islamic Revolutionary Court on 15 March 1979, and on 7 April he was tried again before the tribunal of Sadegh Khalkhali, a cleric. The indictment had about twenty counts, among them spreading corruption on earth, fighting God, sedition and membership of the Freemasons, and many of them were never supported by evidence. Khalkhali refused to let him testify and insulted him, and Hoveyda, who called the court a sham, stopped defending himself.

## Aftermath

Khalkhali sentenced Hoveyda to death by firing squad and confiscation of his property. Minutes later he was shot. The execution of the former prime minister was followed by those of many other officials of the old regime, and the trial is often cited as an example of the summary justice of the first months of the revolution.
