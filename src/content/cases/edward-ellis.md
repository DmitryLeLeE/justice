## The case

Edward S. Ellis was a deputy street superintendent in Los Angeles. In 1904 he stood trial on charges of accepting bribes to sign off on public works without inspecting them.

Everything this file records comes from the Commons description of the courtroom sketch: the charge, the hung jury and the mistrial on 31 December 1904, and the two women in the foreground, Ellis's wife and mother. It is a minor local case, included for the drawing.

## What the file holds

This is the thinnest record on the site, and it is kept here for the drawing. The charge, the failure of the jury to agree and the mistrial on the last day of 1904 are all taken from the description that accompanies the sketch. The picture shows two women in the foreground, identified there as Ellis's wife and his mother, sitting close to the dock.

## Why it is here

Most of the cases on this site changed a law or an era. This one did not. It is a reminder that the courtroom artist drew the ordinary business of the municipal court as well as the great trials, and that a hung jury in a bribery case could be a three-line item in a newspaper.
