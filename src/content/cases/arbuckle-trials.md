## The case

Roscoe "Fatty" Arbuckle was one of the first great stars of American silent comedy. On 5 September 1921 he held a party in a suite at the St. Francis Hotel in San Francisco over the Labor Day weekend. A young actress, Virginia Rappe, fell ill during the party and died four days later of peritonitis from a ruptured bladder. A friend of hers, Maude Delmont, told the police that Arbuckle had raped her, and the story spread across the newspapers, many of which ran lurid and false versions.

## In the courtroom

Arbuckle was tried three times for manslaughter. The first jury, in the winter of 1921, split ten to two for conviction, and the second, early in 1922, ten to two for acquittal. Delmont never testified, since the district attorney thought her unreliable, and the defence showed that the prosecution had pressed witnesses. At the third trial, in April 1922, Arbuckle took the stand himself. The jury was out for six minutes and returned a written statement saying that the acquittal was not enough and that a great injustice had been done to him.

## Aftermath

The Hollywood studios, afraid of the scandal, had pulled his films and then, through the new censor Will Hays, banned him from the screen; the ban was lifted at the end of 1922, but he worked mostly as a director under a pseudonym and died in 1933.
