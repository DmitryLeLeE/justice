## The case

Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, who had been president and then prime minister of Pakistan, was removed by the army chief General Zia-ul-Haq on 5 July 1977, two days after his government had agreed to hold new elections under a neutral caretaker administration. He was later charged with having ordered the killing of Nawab Muhammad Ahmed Khan Kasuri, the father of a political opponent, whose car had been ambushed in Lahore in November 1974.

## In the courtroom

Under the military government the case was heard at first instance by the Lahore High Court, before a bench led by Chief Justice Maulvi Mushtaq Hussain. The prosecution's case rested on the evidence of a former head of the Federal Security Force who had turned approver. Bhutto's supporters said that the judges were hostile and that the evidence had been procured. On 18 March 1978 the court sentenced him to death. The Supreme Court heard the appeal and on 6 February 1979 upheld the sentence by four votes to three.

## Aftermath

Leaders from many countries appealed for clemency, but Zia refused, and Bhutto was hanged at the district jail in Rawalpindi on 4 April 1979. The trial was widely called a judicial murder. In 2024 the Supreme Court of Pakistan, answering a presidential reference, said that the proceedings had not met the requirements of a fair trial. His daughter Benazir Bhutto later became prime minister twice.
