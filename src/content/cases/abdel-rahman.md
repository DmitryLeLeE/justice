## The case

The blind Egyptian cleric and nine others were tried in New York on charges of seditious conspiracy, including a plot to bomb New York landmarks.

The Sheikh and his co-defendants were convicted in October 1995. He was sentenced to life imprisonment in January 1996.

## In the courtroom

The trial ran for months in a heavily guarded Manhattan courtroom. The Sheikh, blind and diabetic, sat among a crowd of co-defendants, and Marilyn Church's drawing shows how crowded the defence side was. Prosecutors relied largely on secretly recorded conversations and on the testimony of an informant, Emad Salem, who had worked with the FBI inside the group.

The government never claimed that the Sheikh had built a bomb. The charge was that he led a conspiracy and gave religious sanction to violence. That was a hard case to put to a jury, and it made the speeches and tapes the heart of the evidence.

## Aftermath

The conviction and the life sentence were upheld on appeal. Abdel Rahman died in prison in February 2017. His lawyer, Lynne Stewart, was later convicted herself for passing messages from him to his followers.
