## The case

The photographs taken at Abu Ghraib in late 2003 showed American soldiers abusing Iraqi detainees. England, a clerk in the military police unit, appears in several of them, including one with a prisoner on a leash. Charles Graner, another soldier in the unit, had already been convicted and sentenced to ten years at Fort Hood in January 2005.

## In the courtroom

England pleaded guilty on 3 May 2005 at Fort Hood. In the plea inquiry Graner, called as a witness, gave testimony that contradicted her account of her own state of mind, and Colonel James Pohl, the military judge, declared a mistrial and threw out the plea. The charges were reduced for a second trial in September 2005.

Pat Lopez drew her on the first day, seated between her civilian lawyer, Rick Hernandez, and her military lawyer, Captain Jonathan Crisp, with the prosecution table opposite.

## Aftermath

She was sentenced to three years and a dishonourable discharge.

## 18+ · The photographs

The pictures taken at Abu Ghraib in late 2003 showed naked prisoners piled into a pyramid, a hooded man standing on a box with wires attached to his hands, and detainees forced into sexual poses. In one, England held a leash that was tied around the neck of a naked man lying on the floor. Her conviction rested on those photographs; the case also showed that the abuse had gone on across several cell blocks and that higher officers were never prosecuted.
