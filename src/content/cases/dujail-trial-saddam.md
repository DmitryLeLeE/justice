## The case

After Saddam Hussein's government fell in 2003 he was captured in December of that year, and in 2004 he was handed to Iraqi authorities. The first case was brought over Dujail, a Shia town north of Baghdad, where in July 1982 gunmen had fired at his motorcade. In reprisal, around 148 people from the town were executed after being tried by a revolutionary court, and many others were detained or tortured and their land destroyed.

## In the courtroom

The trial before the Iraqi High Tribunal opened in October 2005. Saddam rejected the authority of the court, disrupted the sessions and went on a hunger strike. Several defence lawyers were shot dead during the trial, and the first presiding judge resigned in January 2006 after criticism that he was too lenient. Human rights groups said the trial fell short of international standards. The documentary case rested on papers signed by Saddam that approved the death sentences.

The Tribunal sentenced him, and two of his co-defendants, to death on 5 November 2006.

## Aftermath

An appeals panel confirmed the sentence in December. Saddam was hanged at a military base in Baghdad on 30 December 2006. A mobile-phone recording of the execution, in which he was taunted by some of those present, circulated widely. Barzan al-Tikriti and Awad al-Bandar were hanged in January 2007; Taha Yassin Ramadan was hanged in March 2007.
