## The case

On the night of 20 August 1989 José Menendez, an entertainment executive, and his wife Kitty were shot to death with shotguns in the den of their house in Beverly Hills. Their sons, Lyle, 21, and Erik, 18, called the police and said that they had come home to find them dead. For months they spent lavishly. In March 1990 Lyle was arrested and Erik surrendered after his psychologist's lover gave the police tapes of therapy sessions in which he had talked of the killings.

## In the courtroom

The first trial, which Court TV broadcast, began in 1993 with one jury for each brother. The brothers did not deny that they had fired the shots. Their lawyers, among them Leslie Abramson, said that they had killed in fear after years of sexual and physical abuse by their father, and that their mother had failed to protect them. The prosecution said that the story was an invention and that they had killed to inherit a fortune. Both juries deadlocked in January 1994.

At the second trial, in 1995, before one jury, Judge Stanley Weisberg barred most of the abuse evidence, and the jury convicted both brothers of first-degree murder on 20 March 1996. They were sentenced to life without parole.

## Aftermath

The case came back to public attention with a documentary series and a dramatisation in 2024. In May 2025 a Los Angeles judge resentenced them to 50 years to life with parole eligibility, and the parole board declined to release them in August of that year.
