Charles Guiteau shot President James Garfield at a Washington railway station on 2 July 1881. Garfield died on 19 September, and the trial opened that November.

Guiteau's lawyers argued that he was insane. Guiteau himself behaved erratically in court, interrupting witnesses and addressing the judge. The trial was reported around the world, and the illustrated press, including European weeklies, printed scenes of the courtroom.

The jury rejected the insanity defence and found him guilty. He was hanged in June 1882.
