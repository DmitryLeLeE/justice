## The case

In June 1871 General Ketchum, a retired Army officer, fell ill as a guest of Elizabeth Wharton, a well-known Baltimore widow. He died, and another guest, Eugene Van Ness, became seriously ill. Police and doctors found that Ketchum had been poisoned with tartar emetic, and the press named her "the Baltimore Borgia". The prosecution said she owed Ketchum money.

## In the courtroom

The defence moved the trial from Baltimore to Annapolis. The case turned on medical evidence about the cause of Ketchum's death. The defence argued that Ketchum had died of natural causes and that the case was only circumstantial.

James E. Taylor, a special artist for Frank Leslie's Illustrated Newspaper, sketched the courtroom.

## Aftermath

The jury found her not guilty.
