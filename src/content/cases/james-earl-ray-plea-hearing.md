## The case

Ray was arrested in London in June 1968 and extradited to Tennessee. On 10 March 1969 he pleaded guilty to the murder of Dr. King in Memphis, a plea that avoided a trial and a possible death sentence. Judge W. Preston Battle empanelled a jury, which included two black men, to hear an outline of the case and accept the 99-year term. Howard Brodie's drawing of that jury is in the Library of Congress.

## In the courtroom

Within days Ray retracted the plea. In October 1974 he came before a federal judge in Memphis to argue that his lawyer, Percy Foreman, had been poorly serving him, had profited from a book contract with the writer William Bradford Huie, and had misled him about the consequences of pleading guilty.

Aggie Kenny travelled to Memphis to cover the hearing and drew Ray seated with his leg crossed over his knee.

## Aftermath

Judge McRae rejected the claim in 1975. Ray escaped from a Tennessee prison for a short time in 1977 and was recaptured. He died in prison in 1998. King's family and others have long questioned whether he acted alone.
