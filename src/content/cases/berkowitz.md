## The case

Between 1976 and 1977 a gunman shot a series of couples and young women in New York City, killing six people and writing letters to the press and police signed "Son of Sam". David Berkowitz was arrested in August 1977.

The case ended in a guilty plea in 1978 rather than a jury trial. Berkowitz repeatedly shouted in the courtroom. The artist Joseph Papin recorded one such scene in the drawing used here.

## In the courtroom

By the time the case reached court the killer had already been caught, and the argument was about his mind. Psychiatrists found him fit to stand trial, and he pleaded guilty. At the sentencing hearing in May 1978 he shouted obscenities, and the New York Daily News put the drawing on its front page next morning.

## Aftermath

The "Son of Sam" letters to the press and the fear of the summer of 1977 changed how New York talked about itself. The state passed a "Son of Sam" law to keep criminals from selling their stories, and the Supreme Court struck down the original version in 1991. Berkowitz later said that his claim of demonic orders was invented, and he has long been refused parole.
