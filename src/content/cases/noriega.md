## The case

The deposed Panamanian leader, captured in the 1989 US invasion, was tried in Miami on drug-trafficking charges.

Noriega was convicted in April 1992 and sentenced to 40 years in prison.

## In the courtroom

Noriega had once been paid by the CIA, and his defence argued that the American government had known of and used his dealings with the Medellín cartel. The judge limited what the defence could say about this. Prosecutors relied on convicted traffickers who testified in return for reduced sentences.

After the conviction Judge Hoeveler also ruled that Noriega was a prisoner of war under the Geneva Conventions.

## Aftermath

The sentence was later cut and he left the United States in 2010 for France, where he was convicted of laundering money. He was sent home to Panama in 2011 and died in 2017.
