## The case

After the monarchy fell in 1792 the royal family was imprisoned in the Temple tower. Louis XVI was tried and executed in January 1793. Marie Antoinette, who was Austrian by birth, was separated from her son in July and moved on 2 August to the Conciergerie, the prison under the courts. The Revolution was at war with Austria and under internal threat, and the Committee of Public Safety decided to try her.

## In the courtroom

The trial before the Revolutionary Tribunal began on 14 October 1793. The prosecutor, Antoine Fouquier-Tinville, accused her of squandering the treasury, of communicating with Austria and other enemies, and of helping to plot against the Republic. Her two lawyers were named only a day or two before the trial and had little time to prepare. More than forty witnesses were called.

Hébert, a radical journalist, added a charge of abusing her young son, based on statements taken from the boy. She refused to answer it directly and appealed to all mothers present, which was reported to have moved the crowd. After two days and a night the jury found her guilty of all the main charges, and she was sentenced to death.

## Aftermath

She was taken in an open cart to the Place de la Révolution, now the Place de la Concorde, and guillotined at about noon on 16 October 1793, at the age of 37. Fouquier-Tinville was himself tried and guillotined in 1795.
