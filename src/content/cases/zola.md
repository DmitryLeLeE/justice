## The case

On 13 January 1898 Zola's open letter "J'accuse...!" appeared on the front page of the newspaper L'Aurore, accusing the army's leadership of covering up the injustice done to Captain Dreyfus and of acquitting Major Esterhazy on orders. The army's response was to bring Zola himself to court for libel.

The trial before the Seine Assize Court ran from 7 to 23 February 1898. The press covered it daily, with engraved illustrations. Zola was found guilty and sentenced to a year in prison and a fine.

The Court of Cassation set the verdict aside on 2 April 1898 on a procedural defect, and a second trial was held in July. On 19 July Zola left for London rather than face a near-certain second conviction.

## In the courtroom

The government had charged Zola with libel on only a few sentences of his letter, so as to keep the facts of the Dreyfus case out of court. President Delegorgue repeatedly ruled questions out of order with the phrase "the question will not be put". The defence called a long list of witnesses all the same, and for two weeks the newspapers carried the proceedings.

Crowds outside shouted against Zola and for the army. He was convicted on 23 February and received the maximum, one year and a fine of 3,000 francs. Perrenx was given four months.

## Aftermath

After the quashing and the second trial at Versailles, Zola stayed in England for eleven months. He returned when the Dreyfus case was reopened, and the 1900 amnesty ended the case. He died in 1902.
