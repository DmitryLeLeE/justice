## The case

George IV and Caroline had lived apart since shortly after the birth of their daughter in 1796. When he became king he refused to have her crowned and set up an inquiry into her conduct during her years abroad, in particular her relationship with her courier Bartolomeo Pergami. The government introduced a bill to dissolve the marriage and strip her of her title.

## In the courtroom

The Lords sat as a court for almost three months, hearing Italian servants and sailors brought over at government expense. Henry Brougham, Caroline's counsel, cross-examined them with great skill and, in his closing speech, hinted that he might tell what he knew about the king. The queen sat in the chamber each day to the cheers of London crowds.

The print used here shows the House of Lords during the proceedings.

## Aftermath

The bill passed by a majority too small to be carried through the Commons, and the Prime Minister withdrew it. In July 1821 she tried to enter Westminster Abbey for the coronation and was turned away at the door. She died three weeks later.
