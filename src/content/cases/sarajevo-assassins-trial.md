## The case

On 28 June 1914 Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie were shot dead in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip, a 19-year-old Bosnian Serb. An earlier bomb thrown by Nedeljko Čabrinović had missed the car. The young men were members of a nationalist group, Young Bosnia, and had been armed and helped across the border by Serbian officers and Serbian-based networks. Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia a month later.

## In the courtroom

Twenty-five people, among them helpers who had hidden weapons or taken part in the planning, were tried in Sarajevo in October 1914 before a court headed by Judge Alois Curinaldi. Princip and Čabrinović did not deny what they had done. Princip said that his aim was to free Bosnia from Austrian rule, and said that he regretted that the Archduchess had been hit, as he had meant to shoot the governor. Under Austrian law a person under 20 at the time of the crime could not be executed, so Princip, Čabrinović and Trifko Grabež received the maximum of twenty years.

## Aftermath

Danilo Ilić, who had organised the group and distributed the weapons, and two helpers, Veljko Čubrilović and Miško Jovanović, were hanged on 3 February 1915. Princip was held in harsh conditions in the fortress of Theresienstadt, lost an arm to bone tuberculosis and died on 28 April 1918.
