## The case

On 5 November 2009 Major Nidal Hasan, an Army psychiatrist who was about to be deployed to Afghanistan, opened fire at the processing centre at Fort Hood, Texas, killing thirteen people and wounding more than thirty. Police officers shot and wounded him, and he was left paralysed from the waist down.

## In the hearing

The Article 32 hearing in October 2010 decides whether charges go forward to a court-martial. Pat Lopez drew the defence table. The Library of Congress describes Hasan as wan beside his lively lawyer, the retired Colonel John Galligan, a former military judge.

## The court-martial

The case came to trial in August 2013. Hasan, who by then had dismissed his lawyers, represented himself and called no witnesses. A panel of military officers found him guilty on every count and sentenced him to death. The Army never called the attack an act of terrorism, a point that the victims' families criticised, and the soldiers who were wounded or killed were later made eligible for the Purple Heart.
