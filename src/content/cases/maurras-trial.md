## The case

Maurras had led Action française for decades and shaped the French nationalist right with his monarchism and anti-Semitism. Under the Occupation he supported Pétain and the Vichy government in his daily paper. After the Liberation he was arrested and charged with having furnished intelligence to the enemy, in other words with writing in favour of collaboration and with calling for denunciations.

## In the courtroom

The trial in Lyon lasted only a few days. Maurras, then seventy-six and almost deaf, defended his work as that of a patriot. When the sentence was passed, he is reported to have said it was the revenge of Dreyfus. The drawing here is by Jean Auscher and shows him at the hearing.

## Aftermath

He was expelled from the Académie française, to which he had been elected in 1938. In prison he went on writing. He was moved to a clinic in 1952 and died eight months after his release.
