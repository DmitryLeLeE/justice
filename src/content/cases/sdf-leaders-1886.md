On 8 February 1886 a meeting of the unemployed in Trafalgar Square, organised by the Social Democratic Federation, was followed by a march through the West End that turned into a riot.

The Federation's leaders, including H. M. Hyndman and John Burns, were arrested and examined at Bow Street, where the Illustrated London News sent an artist to sketch the hearing. They were tried at the Old Bailey in April 1886 and acquitted.
