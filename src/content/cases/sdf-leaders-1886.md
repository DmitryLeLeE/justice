## The case

On 8 February 1886 a meeting of the unemployed in Trafalgar Square, organised by the Social Democratic Federation, was followed by a march through the West End that turned into a riot.

The Federation's leaders, including H. M. Hyndman and John Burns, were arrested and examined at Bow Street, where the Illustrated London News sent an artist to sketch the hearing. They were tried at the Old Bailey in April 1886 and acquitted.

## At Bow Street

The riot of 8 February broke windows in the clubs of Pall Mall and frightened the West End. Within a week the government prosecuted the Federation's leaders, who had called the meeting. The Illustrated London News showed the magistrates' hearing with the four men standing at the bar.

## Aftermath

At the Old Bailey the case rested on whether the leaders had meant to cause a riot. The jury acquitted them. Burns defended himself with great effect, and the acquittal was a political success for the young socialist movement; three years later, in 1889, he took a leading part in the London Dock Strike.
