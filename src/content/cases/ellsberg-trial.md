## The case

Daniel Ellsberg and Anthony Russo were tried in Los Angeles for leaking the Pentagon Papers. The drawing dates from 14 July 1972.

Judge William Byrne dismissed all charges on 11 May 1973 because of government misconduct, including the break-in at Ellsberg's psychiatrist's office.

## In the courtroom

Ellsberg, a former Pentagon analyst, had copied a secret history of the Vietnam War and given it to the press. The charges carried up to 115 years. The defence tried to argue why he did it; the judge largely kept those arguments from the jury.

Then the government began to unravel. In April 1973 Judge Byrne announced that the White House "Plumbers" had broken into the office of Ellsberg's psychiatrist, Lewis Fielding, in 1971. The sketch here, signed Rose, dates from a hearing in July 1972, months before the break-in came to light. Then came word that the judge had been approached by John Ehrlichman about a job as FBI director while the trial was on, and that wiretap records had gone missing.

## Aftermath

On 11 May 1973 Byrne dismissed the case, saying the government's conduct had made a fair verdict impossible. The break-in became one of the strands that led to Watergate, and the case is still a reference point for debates about leaks and the First Amendment.
