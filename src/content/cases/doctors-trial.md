## The case

The first of twelve trials held by American authorities at Nuremberg after the main International Military Tribunal, this one put on trial 20 physicians and three officials. They were charged with planning and carrying out medical experiments on prisoners in concentration camps without consent, with the murder of disabled people and of others in the "euthanasia" programme, and with membership of the SS, which the earlier tribunal had declared a criminal organisation. Karl Brandt, Hitler's personal physician and head of the programme, was the first named defendant.

## In the courtroom

The prosecution, led by Telford Taylor, opened on 9 December 1946 and presented documents, many of them written by the defendants, and testimony from survivors of the experiments. The defence argued that the experiments had been authorised by the state and were comparable to research done elsewhere, and that some prisoners had volunteered. The judges rejected both arguments, and in their judgment set out ten principles for lawful research on human subjects, now known as the Nuremberg Code, of which the first is that the voluntary consent of the subject is essential.

Of the 23 accused, seven were acquitted, seven condemned to death, five given life imprisonment and four sentenced to terms of between 10 and 20 years.

## Aftermath

Brandt and six others were hanged at Landsberg on 2 June 1948. The Nuremberg Code was a base for later declarations on research ethics, among them the Declaration of Helsinki, and the trial is a standard case in the study of medical ethics.

## 18+ · What the experiments were

The evidence covered high-altitude tests in low-pressure chambers, in which prisoners at Dachau died, freezing experiments in which people were immersed in icy water, and experiments in which wounds were infected with bacteria or filled with glass and wood to test sulfonamide drugs. Prisoners were deliberately infected with typhus, malaria and other diseases to test vaccines, and in other experiments they were sterilised or poisoned. Survivors who testified described the pain and the permanent injuries. The tribunal found that many of the victims died and that others were killed afterwards so that their bodies could be examined.
