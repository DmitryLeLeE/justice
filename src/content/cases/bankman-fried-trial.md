## The case

Sam Bankman-Fried founded the cryptocurrency exchange FTX in 2019 and its sister trading firm, Alameda Research. By 2022 FTX was one of the largest exchanges in the world and he was a prominent donor to political causes. In November 2022 a report that Alameda held a large amount of FTX's own token set off a run on the exchange, which could not repay customers. It filed for bankruptcy within days, and prosecutors in New York said that Bankman-Fried had secretly lent customers' deposits to Alameda to cover its losses.

## In the courtroom

The trial before Judge Lewis Kaplan began on 3 October 2023 and lasted four weeks. The key witnesses were three of his former associates, including Caroline Ellison, the former head of Alameda, who had pleaded guilty and described how she and Bankman-Fried had agreed to use customers' money. He took the stand himself and said that he had not intended to defraud anyone. The jury took less than five hours to convict him on all seven counts.

## Aftermath

Judge Kaplan sentenced him to 25 years, and said that Bankman-Fried had perjured himself. Customers of the exchange are expected to recover most of their claims through the bankruptcy, in dollar terms as of the date of filing. His appeal continues.
