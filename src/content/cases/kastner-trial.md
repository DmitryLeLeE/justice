## The case

Rudolf Kastner, a Hungarian Zionist lawyer and journalist, had negotiated in Budapest in 1944 with SS officers, among them Adolf Eichmann and Kurt Becher, for the lives of Jews. A train that left Hungary that year carried about 1,684 Jews, some chosen by Kastner and his committee, to safety in Switzerland in exchange for money and valuables. After the war he moved to Israel, worked for the government and the ruling Mapai party, and testified at Nuremberg for Becher. In 1953 Malchiel Gruenwald, a Jerusalem pensioner, published a pamphlet accusing him of collaborating with the Nazis and of saving his own friends and relatives while keeping the others in the dark.

## In the courtroom

The government decided to prosecute Gruenwald for criminal libel, in Kastner's name. The case was heard in the Jerusalem District Court. Gruenwald's young lawyer, Shmuel Tamir, made the trial an attack on Kastner and the Mapai leadership, and Kastner became the defendant in everything but name. In 1955 Judge Benjamin Halevi cleared Gruenwald of most of the charge and wrote that Kastner had "sold his soul to the devil". The government, which had prosecuted the case, lost a vote of confidence tied to it and Prime Minister Moshe Sharett resigned.

## Aftermath

Kastner was shot outside his home in Tel Aviv in March 1957 and died on 15 March. The Supreme Court, in January 1958, overturned most of Halevi's verdict, but unanimously upheld the finding about Kastner's help for the SS officer Becher after the war. The case still divides Israelis and historians over what leaders of Jewish communities could and should have done in 1944.
