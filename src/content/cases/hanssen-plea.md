## The case

Robert Hanssen joined the FBI in 1976. From 1979, with a long break, he sold the Soviet Union and then Russia thousands of pages of classified documents, among them the identities of agents who were spying for the United States. At least two, Dmitri Polyakov and Valery Martynov, were executed. He took about $1.4 million in cash and diamonds and never met his handlers; he used dead drops under a false name.

The FBI came to him after buying a file on the mole from a Russian source, which contained a plastic bag with his fingerprints and a recording of his voice. He was arrested on 18 February 2001 after leaving a package at a dead drop in a park near his Virginia home.

## In the courtroom

There was no trial. In July 2001 Hanssen pleaded guilty to 15 counts under an agreement that spared him the death penalty in exchange for full cooperation in a debrief. At the sentencing hearing in the Alexandria courthouse on 10 May 2002, Judge Claude Hilton, who had also sentenced Aldrich Ames, imposed life without parole.

## Aftermath

An inquiry by the FBI led to changes in its handling of counterintelligence. Hanssen, who was held in solitary confinement at the federal supermax prison in Florence, Colorado, died in June 2023 aged 79.
