## The case

In 1899, as the Dreyfus case was reopened, the government charged leading nationalist and royalist figures, including Paul Déroulède, André Buffet and Jules Guérin, with conspiring against the Republic. The Senate, constituted as the High Court of Justice, tried them in the Luxembourg Palace from the autumn.

Le Monde illustré ran sketches by Louis Malteste of the defendants and their lawyers in December 1899. The verdict came in January 1900.

## In the courtroom

The Senate, sitting as the High Court of Justice in the Luxembourg Palace, tried the leaders of the nationalist and royalist leagues for plotting against the Republic. The accused were noisy. Guérin, whose men had held out for weeks in a house in the rue de Chabrol, was thrown out of the hearing for insults and given a prison term for contempt, which was folded into his final sentence.

The sketch reproduced here, by Louis Malteste for the Monde illustré, shows one of the accused, M. Brunet, with his lawyer Jacquemont.

## Aftermath

Déroulède and Buffet were banished for ten years, Guérin was given ten years of detention and Habert five years of banishment. The sentences were not the main point. The Waldeck-Rousseau government used the trial to break the leagues, and the nationalists did not threaten the Republic again in the same way before 1914.
