## The case

The Ligue des patriotes, founded by Paul Déroulède, was a nationalist league that backed General Boulanger in the late 1880s. As the government moved against Boulanger's movement in spring 1889, it also took its supporters to court.

Le Figaro printed a sketch of the hearing by Eugène Rapp on 3 April 1889. The leaders were each fined 100 francs. The League had been dissolved, though Déroulède returned to politics and later featured in the Dreyfus Affair.

## In the courtroom

The government of the day was afraid of General Boulanger, the popular soldier-politician who had come close to a coup in January 1889, and the League of Patriots was his most visible ally. The prosecution accused the League of being a secret association, forbidden by law.

The trial was held two days after Boulanger had escaped to Belgium, which gave the case a weakened air. Déroulède used the hearing to deliver speeches. Eugène Rapp's sketch in Le Figaro shows the defendants lined up.

## Aftermath

The fines were symbolic and the League was ordered closed. It reformed after a few years, and in 1899 its leaders were back in a courtroom, this time before the High Court of the Senate.
