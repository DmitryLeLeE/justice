## The case

Guillaume Bernays, an Antwerp lawyer, was lured to a house at 159 rue de la Loi in Brussels by a man who called himself Henry Vaughan, supposedly a businessman with a legal matter. He was shot in the neck. The brothers Léon and Armand Peltzer had devised the plot. Armand was infatuated with Bernays's wife Julie and thought that her husband's death was the only way to win her. Léon, playing Vaughan, was traced after a pharmacist in Verviers recognised his handwriting.

## In the courtroom

The investigation took about ten months and heard 107 witnesses. The trial was a sensation in Brussels, with leading counsel such as Edmond Picard and Jules Lejeune. The jury found both brothers fully responsible and they were sentenced to death. The sentence was commuted to life because the motive was passion.

## Aftermath

Armand died in prison soon afterwards. Léon was freed at the age of 65 and left for London under another name. He drowned in the North Sea in 1922, probably by suicide. Julie Bernays was cleared of suspicion and became a journalist. The affair inspired plays, a novel and a film in 1934.
