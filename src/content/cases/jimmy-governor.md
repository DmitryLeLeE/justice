## The case

On 20 July 1900, at Breelong in central New South Wales, Jimmy Governor and a companion killed members of the Mawbey family and a teacher, Ellen Kerz, after insults directed at Governor's white wife Ethel. Over the next week there were further killings near Ulan, Merriwa and Wollar, nine deaths in all. For three months Governor and his brother Joe were hunted by police and civilians across the ranges. Governor was shot and captured in October, and Joe was killed days later.

## In the courtroom

He was tried at the Central Criminal Court in Darlinghurst before Mr Justice Owen, beginning on 22 November 1900. The jury found him guilty of the murder of Kerz the next day and the judge sentenced him to death.

The Sydney Evening News printed an illustration of Governor on the day he was hanged.

## Aftermath

The case became the basis of Thomas Keneally's 1972 novel "The Chant of Jimmie Blacksmith" and of Fred Schepisi's film of 1978. It is still discussed as a story of race and the frontier.
