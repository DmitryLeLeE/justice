## The case

In the West Port district of Edinburgh in 1828, William Burke and William Hare, with their partners, killed lodgers and acquaintances by smothering and sold the bodies to the anatomist Robert Knox. Knox's school paid well and asked no questions. Sixteen victims are usually counted. They were found out when two lodgers discovered the body of Margaret Docherty under a bed in Hare's house.

## In the courtroom

The Crown could not prove its case without insider evidence, and the Lord Advocate offered Hare immunity if he told the truth. Hare and his wife gave evidence against Burke, and the trial lasted a night and a day. Burke was found guilty of Docherty's murder. His partner Helen M'Dougal got the Scottish verdict of "not proven".

The sketch shown here was taken in court during the trial.

## Aftermath

Burke was hanged and his body dissected in public, as the judge had ordered. Thousands came to see his corpse. Hare was released in 1829 and disappeared. Knox was never charged, but his reputation was ruined. The scandal helped to bring about the Anatomy Act of 1832, which gave medical schools a legal supply of bodies.
