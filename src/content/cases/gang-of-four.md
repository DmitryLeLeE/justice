## The case

The Special Court was created by a decision of China's National People's Congress on 29 September 1980. The dock held four prominent figures of the Cultural Revolution era, including Mao's widow, arrested in October 1976, and six men from the circle of Lin Biao, who died in 1971. The hearings ran from 20 November 1980 to 25 January 1981.

Jiang Qing was defiant: she shouted at witnesses and judges and claimed she had acted on Mao's direct instructions. Zhang Chunqiao stayed silent and refused to admit guilt. Two other defendants expressed remorse and received lighter sentences. The remaining six received sentences of 16 to 18 years.

The historian Alexander Cook calls the trial instructive: the authorities wanted to show that the arbitrariness of the Cultural Revolution should give way to the rule of law. The verdict could not be appealed.

## In the courtroom

The proceedings were designed to turn a page. The court tried the four and the Lin Biao group together to place on them the blame for the Cultural Revolution, while leaving Mao's own responsibility outside the courtroom. Parts of the hearings were shown on state television, and the audience was told that the law, not the party's whim, now decided the matter.

Jiang Qing refused to cooperate and shouted at the judges. Zhang Chunqiao kept silent. Wang Hongwen and Yao Wenyuan said they were sorry.

## Sentences

On 25 January 1981 Jiang Qing and Zhang Chunqiao were sentenced to death with a two-year suspension, which was commuted to life imprisonment in 1983. Wang Hongwen received life, and Yao Wenyuan twenty years. Among the Lin Biao group, Chen Boda, Huang Yongsheng, Wu Faxian, Li Zuopeng, Qiu Huizuo and Jiang Tengjiao received sentences between 16 and 18 years.

## Aftermath

Jiang Qing was released on medical grounds and died in 1991, officially by suicide. Zhang died in 2005 and Yao, freed in 1996, died the same year.
