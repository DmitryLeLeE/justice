## The case

Terrence Lakin, an Army flight surgeon, refused orders to deploy to Afghanistan in 2010. He said he would not go until the president's eligibility for office was proven. The claim he relied on, a birth-certificate conspiracy theory, had been repeatedly rejected.

The court-martial met at Fort Meade in December 2010. The judge ruled that the question of the president's eligibility was not a defence to disobeying a lawful order. The Army released an illustration of the proceedings, shown here.

## In the courtroom

Lakin, a career officer with eighteen years of service, had made a public video saying that he would not obey orders until the president's eligibility was proved. The court-martial was short. The military judge held that the president's birthplace had no bearing on whether an order to deploy was lawful, and she barred the defence from the evidence it wanted to use.

The illustration shown here was made by Staff Sgt. Amy Brown of the US Army and shows Lakin with members of the panel at Fort Meade on 15 December 2010. It is an official sketch, not the work of a press artist.

## Aftermath

Lakin was sentenced to six months in prison and dismissed from the service, and he served his term at Fort Leavenworth. The "birther" claim had been rejected in many courts, and the trial ended any hope that a military case could be used to test it.
