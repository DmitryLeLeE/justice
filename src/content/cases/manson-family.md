## The case

Charles Manson and three followers were tried in Los Angeles for the 1969 Tate and LaBianca murders. The drawing shows Manson leaping towards Judge Charles Older on 5 October 1970.

Manson and his co-defendants were convicted in January 1971. Their death sentences were later converted to life imprisonment.

## In the courtroom

The trial lasted nine months and was disrupted by the defendants. On 5 October 1970 Manson jumped from the table at the judge with a sharpened pencil, shouting that the court had no right to try him. Bill Robles caught the moment from the press seats. His drawing of that day is the best-known image of the trial.

The prosecution's case was that Manson had directed the killings, in order to start a race war which he called "Helter Skelter". Manson never took part in the murders himself, and this was the difficulty that Bugliosi had to solve with the testimony of a former Family member, Linda Kasabian.

## Aftermath

The jury sat for almost two days before convicting all four. Manson died in prison in 2017.

## 18+ · The nights of 8 and 9 August 1969

Seven people died in two nights. At the Cielo Drive house the followers of Manson killed Sharon Tate, who was eight and a half months pregnant, with the coffee heiress Abigail Folger, the writer Wojciech Frykowski, the hairdresser Jay Sebring and a visitor, 18-year-old Steven Parent, who had been shot in his car. The word "PIGS" was written on the front door in Tate's blood. The next night Leno and Rosemary LaBianca were stabbed to death in their own home. Bugliosi told the jury that Manson wanted to start a race war that he called "Helter Skelter", and that he had sent his followers to do the killing.
