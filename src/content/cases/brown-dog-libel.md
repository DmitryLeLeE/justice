In 1903 the anti-vivisection campaigner Stephen Coleridge published a claim that the physiologist William Bayliss had operated on a conscious brown terrier at University College London in front of a lecture audience. Bayliss sued for libel.

The trial took place in November 1903 and was covered by the illustrated press. The jury sided with Bayliss. The affair did not end there: a bronze memorial to the dog erected in Battersea in 1906 became the target of protests.
