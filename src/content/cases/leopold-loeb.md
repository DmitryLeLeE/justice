## The case

On 21 May 1924 fourteen-year-old Robert Franks was taken off a Chicago street and killed. Within ten days the police had traced a pair of eyeglasses found near the body to Nathan Leopold, a nineteen-year-old law student, and both he and his friend Richard Loeb, eighteen, confessed. They came from rich families and had planned the crime for months, partly as a test of whether they could commit a perfect one.

## In the courtroom

The confessions left the defence no chance of an acquittal, so Clarence Darrow had both men plead guilty and put their fate to the judge, not a jury, in the hope of avoiding the death sentence. The sentencing hearing ran for 32 days, with psychiatrists called by both sides and a long argument about whether the pair were responsible for what they had done. Darrow's closing plea was delivered over three days in August and became one of the best-known speeches in American legal history; he argued against the death penalty and the idea of punishing youth with it.

State's Attorney Crowe asked for the gallows. On 10 September Judge Caverly gave each man life imprisonment for the murder and 99 years for the kidnapping, and said that his decision rested mainly on their youth.

## Aftermath

Loeb was attacked with a razor by another inmate in 1936 and died of his wounds; the inmate was found not guilty. Leopold was paroled in 1958 and died in Puerto Rico in 1971. The case inspired plays and films, among them Rope, Compulsion and Swoon.

## 18+ · The killing

Franks was struck several times on the head with a chisel, gagged, and died in the back seat of the car. The two men hid the body in a culvert near Wolf Lake and poured hydrochloric acid over the face and the genitals so that the boy would be harder to identify. Then they sent his family a ransom demand. Neither showed remorse at the time of the confessions, and the defence accepted that the motive was the thrill of the act.
