The genocide of the Tutsi in Rwanda began on 7 April 1994. Jean-Paul Akayesu was the mayor of the Taba commune. At first he kept the killers out, but after a meeting with leaders of the interim government on 18 April, things changed. The court found that the next day he urged residents to take part in the killings, and that murder, torture and rape followed in the commune.

Akayesu was arrested in Zambia in October 1995 and transferred to Arusha, seat of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, which the UN Security Council had created in 1994. His trial opened on 9 January 1997 and was the tribunal's first on the merits. The case rested on witness testimony; there was no physical evidence.

The judgment entered the case law of international criminal law. For the first time a tribunal interpreted the definition of genocide in the 1948 Convention, and it held that sexual violence can be an act of genocide when it is aimed at destroying a group.
