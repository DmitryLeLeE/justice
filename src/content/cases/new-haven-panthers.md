## The case

The Connecticut artist Robert Templeton drew the participants in the 1971 New Haven trial of Black Panther Party chairman Bobby Seale and organiser Ericka Huggins. They were charged with kidnapping, conspiracy and murder in connection with the killing of Alex Rackley, who had been suspected of being a police informant.

The trial in Connecticut Superior Court began with jury selection in autumn 1970. The jury deadlocked in May 1971, and Judge Harold Mulvey dismissed the charges, saying an impartial jury could not be found again. Templeton's pastel and ink sketches are held at Yale.

The drawing shown here is an overview of the courtroom, with the judge's bench and the defence table.

## In the courtroom

The trial took months to start. Jury selection took far longer than usual, with hundreds of citizens questioned about their views of the Panthers, and was held in a city under the eyes of the national press. A May Day rally in 1970 had brought tens of thousands to the green in support of the defendants.

Robert Templeton, a local portrait painter, sat in court and made pastel sketches, which are now held by the Beinecke Library at Yale.

## Aftermath

The judge said that after the publicity the state could not find an impartial jury and dismissed the case. Others in the group had pleaded guilty to the killing or been convicted of it. The case showed how federal and local police had used informers in the movement, a pattern that came out more fully in the COINTELPRO investigations.
