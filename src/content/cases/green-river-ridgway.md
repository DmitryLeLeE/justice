## The case

From 1982 the bodies of young women, many of them runaways or sex workers, were found along the Green River and in the woods near Seattle. A task force worked for years and had Ridgway under suspicion in 1983 and 1987, when a saliva sample was taken. In 2001, a laboratory used new DNA techniques on old evidence and matched semen from four victims to that sample, and Ridgway was arrested on 30 November 2001, outside the truck factory where he had worked since 1969.

## In the courtroom

In November 2003 Ridgway, who faced the death penalty, agreed to plead guilty to 48 murders in exchange for a sentence of life imprisonment, and promised to help the police find the bodies of other victims. At the sentencing hearing in December, relatives of the dead spoke to him directly, some in anger and some in grief, and Ridgway apologised. Judge Richard Jones imposed 48 consecutive life terms without parole.

## Aftermath

Ridgway later told the police that he had killed 71 women, though he claimed more, and he has been linked to further killings. Several victims were identified years afterwards by DNA work, the latest in the 2010s.

## 18+ · Method

Ridgway said that he strangled the women, most of them after having sex, and left their bodies in the woods and in the river, and sometimes returned to the bodies. He told investigators that he picked sex workers because they were easy to pick up and unlikely to be missed.
