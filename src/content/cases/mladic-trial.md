## The case

Mladić commanded the army of the Republika Srpska during the war in Bosnia and Herzegovina from 1992 to 1995. He was indicted in 1995 by the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia, together with the Bosnian Serb leader Radovan Karadžić, and spent sixteen years in hiding. Serbian police arrested him in a village in the north of the country in May 2011, and he was transferred to The Hague.

## In the courtroom

The trial began on 16 May 2012 and lasted until December 2016, with almost 600 witnesses and thousands of documents. The prosecution said that Mladić had led a campaign to drive non-Serbs from large parts of Bosnia, including the siege of Sarajevo and the killings at Srebrenica, and that he had taken hostages from the UN forces. He was often removed from the courtroom for outbursts, and his health delayed the proceedings. On 22 November 2017 the judges found him guilty of ten of the eleven counts and sentenced him to life imprisonment; he was acquitted on one count of genocide relating to municipalities in Bosnia.

## Aftermath

His appeal was dismissed in June 2021 by the Mechanism for International Criminal Tribunals. Mladić was transferred to a prison in Scheveningen and died in custody in March 2025.

## 18+ · Srebrenica and Sarajevo

After the town of Srebrenica fell in July 1995, Bosnian Serb forces separated men and boys from the women and killed around 8,000 of them over several days, with many of the victims shot and buried in mass graves. The Tribunal found that this was genocide. In Sarajevo, a siege that lasted nearly four years left more than 11,000 people dead, many of them civilians killed by shelling and sniper fire on streets and at markets.
