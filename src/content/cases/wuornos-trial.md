## The case

In the winter of 1989 and 1990 seven men were found shot dead along roads and in woods in central Florida. Several had been robbed. In January 1991 police arrested Aileen Wuornos, who had been living with a female partner in motels, after tracing the victims' cars and property that had been pawned. She confessed and said that Richard Mallory, a man in his fifties, had raped and beaten her, and that she had shot him in self-defence.

## In the courtroom

The first trial, for Mallory's murder, opened in Volusia County in January 1992. The state argued that she had killed to rob him, and was allowed to introduce evidence of the other killings. She testified for the defence, but was a poor witness, and the jury found her guilty after less than two hours; Judge Uriel Blount sentenced her to death. In the following year she pleaded no contest to several more killings and received further death sentences. The body of the seventh victim was never found, and she was not charged in his case.

## Aftermath

At the end she told the courts that she had killed without provocation and wanted no more appeals. She was executed in October 2002. Her story was told in documentaries by the filmmaker Nick Broomfield and in the 2003 film Monster, for which Charlize Theron won an Academy Award.
