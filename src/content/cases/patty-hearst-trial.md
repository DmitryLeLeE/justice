## The case

Patty Hearst, 19, granddaughter of the publisher William Randolph Hearst, was taken from her Berkeley apartment on 4 February 1974 by the Symbionese Liberation Army, a tiny armed group that called itself revolutionary. Two months later she announced that she had joined it, took the name Tania, and on 15 April a security camera filmed her holding a rifle during the robbery of the Hibernia Bank in San Francisco. She spent the next year and a half on the run, and was arrested in September 1975.

## In the courtroom

At the trial in San Francisco in early 1976, her defence, led by F. Lee Bailey, said that she had been kidnapped, confined, threatened and brainwashed, and had acted out of fear. The prosecution argued that she had joined voluntarily, pointing to tapes in which she denounced her family and to her statements after arrest. She did not stand up well under cross-examination: she invoked the Fifth Amendment on dozens of questions. The jury convicted her on 20 March 1976.

## Aftermath

She was sentenced to seven years and served about 22 months, until President Jimmy Carter commuted her sentence in February 1979. President Bill Clinton pardoned her on his last day in office, in January 2001. The case is often cited in discussions of coercion and what is now called coercive control.
