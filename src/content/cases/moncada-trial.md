## The case

On 26 July 1953 about 136 rebels led by Fidel Castro, a 26-year-old lawyer, attacked the Moncada Barracks in Santiago de Cuba, the second-largest army garrison in the country, with a simultaneous attack on the barracks at Bayamo. The plan was to seize arms and call a rising against the dictator Fulgencio Batista. It failed within hours. Several of the rebels were killed in the fighting, and dozens more were executed afterwards by the army.

## In the courtroom

More than a hundred people were indicted, and the survivors were tried in the Palace of Justice in Santiago from 21 September to 6 October 1953, in eleven sessions. Castro, a lawyer, defended himself, and the court later separated his case from the others and heard it separately. He used the hearing to describe what he said were the crimes of the regime. The text he wrote out in prison from that defence, "History Will Absolve Me", was smuggled out and circulated widely. Castro was sentenced to 15 years and Raúl Castro to 13, and they were held in the Presidio Modelo.

## Aftermath

In 1955 the Cuban Congress passed a general amnesty bill, and the imprisoned rebels were freed after Batista signed it. Fidel and Raúl Castro went to Mexico, where they organised the expedition that landed in Cuba in 1956. The movement took its name from the date of the attack, the 26th of July Movement, and Batista fled the country on the last day of 1958.
